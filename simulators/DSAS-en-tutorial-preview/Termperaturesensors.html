For measuring the temperature with adequate accuracy, the most common sensor are Pt100. They are used to measure the temperature of:

•The Generator

•The Gearbox

•The Gearbox oil

•The Rotor bearing

•The ambient

This sensor consist of a Platinum resistor, encapsulated according to the surface or  element to measure its temperature. The Pt100 sensor presents 100 ohms where its temperature is 0ºC, and its evolution with temperature is shown in the next figure:

Pt100 Resistance vs Temperature

The measurement of this low value of ohms requires the use of a 4 wires circuit (see next figure), in order to minimize the effect of resistance of the (possibly) long measuring cables (RC1, RC2, RC3, RC4).

In this circuit, the internal impedance of the "Current Generator" is much higher than the combine series resistance of Rc1 + Pt100 + Rc4. The internal impedance of the miliVoltmeter is much higher than Rc2 + Rc3. Therefore, all the current (I) from the Current Generator passes completely through the Pt100. In this way, the measurement of the miliVoltmeter will be  I * RPt100 , regardless of the length of the cables connecting the Pt100.

A cheaper solution consists of the use of similar cables (length and section) for the connections, in which case the 3 wires circuit can be used. See next figure.

In this scheme, assuming the resistance between T1and A is equal to the resistance between T3 and B, the voltage between T1 and A can be measured between T3 and T2, and can be used to compensated the T1 – T2 measurement.

There is another important aspect relative to the use of Pt100: the current used to measure its resistance (I) does heat it, and that would introduce an error on the measurement of the temperature of the element at which it is attached. Therefore, this current must be reduced to the minimum: the lower limit depends on the sensitivity and signal to noise ratio of the miliVoltmeter. Other solutions include the use of pulsed currents, with very low duty cycle signal, but with high peak value: the high value of the current reduce the requirements for sensitivity of the miliVoltmeter, and the low duty cycle reduces the heating of the Pt100 due to this measuring current. The measuring system is, however, much more complex.
