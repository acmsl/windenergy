The purposes of this synoptic are:


The purposes of the "Wind & Nacelle orientation synoptic" are:

•To permit the user to force wind speed and direction.

•To show the Nacelle orientation.

•To show the number of turns of the cables connecting the generator up in the Nacelle to the Electric Panel at the bottom of the Wind Turbine.

•To show the (simulated) wind vane signal. 

•To show and the orientation motors, the wind vane signal and the power cable turns around the themselves, as well as providing widgets to change the wind speed and direction and manual control of the yawing motors.

The miss-alignment of the brown and blue needles (nacelle's orientation and wind direction), shown in this particular view of the synoptic, gives a direct view of the difference between both signals.

With these two blue widgets you make change the wind speed and the wind direction. The changes can be made either typing in the new desired values (ending with return key) or using both the PageUp/PageDown (changes with 1 m/s and 10 º, respectively) or Arrow keys (Up/Down) (changes with 0.1 m/s and 1º respectively.

The "Nacelle orientation" is presentation only.

As the orientation mechanism try to follow the shifting of the wind direction, the power cables that connect the Generator and Back-to-Back Converters in the Nacelle down to the connection to the main cable connecting to the Point of Power Connection, turn on themselves. There are two limits associated with the number of these turns: one is a software limit, that can be change with the parameter "ControllerYawing.MaxCableTwistForReorientation". Another corresponds to a physical switch located in the Nacelle, that gets actuated (On) when the turns arrives to a absolut maximum limit. If that ever occurs it generated a automatic Stop. This limit can be changed with the parameter "NacelleYawing.MaxCableTwist".

The wind vane is a device that produces a signal from where it is possible to deduct the angle between its absolute orientation and the direction of the wind hitting it. Normally the windvane is fixed at the rear of the Nacelle, so its signal can be used for knowing the disorientation between these two directions: wind direction and Nacelle orientation.

The signal is oscilating continuously and its "average" duty cycle corresponds to the disorientation angle. This diagram represents a real life form of that signal, including the randomness expected from a real wind vane.

These widgets, in the default condition, present the value of the Controller ouput signal corresponding to the yawing motors (to the left and to the right).

They can be set in the "forcing-mode" (press F6), in which case the orientation of the Nacelle can be controlled manual through them. This is a good method to simulate a problem in the yawing mechanism as, when there are in forcing-mode the Controller commands are not obeyed. Thus leaving them in forcing-mode and changing the wind direction will provoque the report of an incidence, as the Controller will try to counteract reorienting the Nacelle and that commands will not be followed the the Nacelle yawing motors.


The bottom part of the right panel shows:

•The Pitch set point made by the Controller, indicated by the pink text.

•The actual pitch of the Blades. This may differs from the Set Point, specially during changes, reflection the limited slew rate of the Pitch mechanism in the Hub.

•The direction and magnitude of the "Incoming wind" (the effective value) indicated by the blue vector going down.

•The direction and magnitude of the "wind rotation" indicated by the black vector: this wind is the wind that the edge of the blade looks due to its rotation.
As the blade is turning leftward (in the figure) the equivalent wind direction is going rightward.
