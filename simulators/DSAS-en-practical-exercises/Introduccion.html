Two-Speed Active Stall Wind Turbine Simulator

Introduction

This tutorial introduces the "Two-Speed Active Stall Wind Turbine Simulator," part of ACM SL's Real-Time Wind Turbine Simulator series.

What does this simulator model?

The simulator recreates wind turbines that use:

  • Squirrel cage generators - a simple, robust type of electric generator, with the stator connected directly to the power grid
  • Active Stall control - a method that controls power output by adjusting blade angles to intentionally reduce their aerodynamic efficiency
  • Two-speed operation - as a method to adapt to an expanded range of wind speeds using two rotational speeds, using a generator with two stator windings, or two generators

How does it work?

The Simulator runs on ACM SL's LdP platform using the "DSASr12.conf" configuration file. Think of LdP as the operating system that brings all the components together.

What can you do with it?

You can:

  • Send the same commands used on real wind turbines and watch how the system responds
  • Observe the behavior of each of the subsystems in real-time, and their interrelations
  • View detailed information from each subsystem (blades, generator, brakes, etc.)
  • Change parameters and signal values in real-time through visual displays called "synoptics"
  • Use it as a Test Bench to experiment safely without risking real equipment

The simulator recreates realistic operating conditions you'd find in an actual wind farm. This means you can safely explore scenarios like:

  • Extreme wind conditions
  • Generator overheating
  • Mechanical failures in the blade pitch system
  • Ice buildup on blades

What type of turbine is simulated?

The wind turbine is:

  • HAWT (Horizontal Axis Wind Turbine) - the blades spin around a horizontal shaft
  • Upwind-oriented - the blades face into the wind
  • Three-bladed - the most common commercial design

What will you learn?

This tutorial explains the physics and engineering principles behind wind turbines that control power using the "stalling" technique—a clever way to limit power output in high winds by reducing blade efficiency.
