Practical Lab 6: Power Control Through Pitch

Objective:

Understand how blade pitch angle controls power extraction across the entire wind speed range. You'll learn:

  • How pitch affects power coefficient (Cp) and power output
  • The complete pitch control strategy from cut-in to cut-out winds
  • Pitch behavior in different operating regions
  • Relationship between pitch, TSR, and Cp
  • Active stall control strategy (our turbine's method)
  • Response to wind gusts and fluctuations
  • Optimization of annual energy production

Background - Pitch as the Primary Control

The Central Control Mechanism:

In a fixed-speed wind turbine, blade pitch is the ONLY way to control power:

What we CAN'T control:

  • ✗ Generator speed (fixed by grid frequency)
  • ✗ Blade rotational speed (fixed by gearbox ratio)
  • ✗ Wind speed (nature controls this!)
  • ✗ Air density (environmental condition)
  • ✗ Swept area (blade length is fixed)

What we CAN control:

  • ✓ Blade pitch angle - and that's it!

The Power Equation (Reminder):

P = 0.5 × ρ × A × V³ × Cp(TSR, Pitch)

Everything else is fixed or environmental except:

  • Cp - which depends on pitch angle!

By changing pitch, we change Cp, which changes power.

The Cp Surface (Reminder)

Let's review the 3D relationship we saw in the Physical Principles section:

Cp = f(TSR, Pitch)

[Reference to 3D Cp surface from earlier]

Key features:

  • Maximum Cp ≈ 0.48 at TSR ≈ 7.5, Pitch ≈ 0°
  • Cp decreases as pitch increases (positive direction)
  • Cp also decreases with negative pitch (into the wind)
  • At pitch = 90° (feathered), Cp ≈ -0.1 (negative!)

For our fixed-speed turbine:

Large Generator (4-pole):

  • Blade speed: ~19.4 RPM (nearly constant)
  • TSR varies with wind: TSR = (2π × 40 × 19.4/60) / V

Operating Regions Overview

The complete wind speed range divides into regions:

Region 1: Below Cut-in (0 - 3.5 m/s)

  • Turbine stopped
  • Not enough wind to produce useful power
  • Pitch: 90° (feathered)
  • Power: 0 kW

Region 2: Partial Load - Small Generator (3.5 - 6.6 m/s)

  • Operating on Small Generator (6-pole)
  • Blade speed: ~13 RPM
  • Pitch: ~0° to +2° (near optimal)
  • Power: 0 - 350 kW (increasing with wind)
  • Goal: Maximize energy capture

Region 3: Partial Load - Large Generator (6.6 - 11 m/s)

  • Operating on Large Generator (4-pole)
  • Blade speed: ~19.4 RPM
  • Pitch: ~0° to +3° (near optimal)
  • Power: 350 - 1,300 kW (increasing with wind)
  • Goal: Maximize energy capture

Region 4: Rated Power (11 - 25 m/s)

  • Operating on Large Generator
  • Pitch: +3° to +20° (increasing with wind)
  • Power: 1,300 kW (constant - limited by generator)
  • Goal: Limit power to rated value

Region 5: Above Cut-out (>25 m/s)

  • Turbine stopped (too dangerous)
  • Pitch: 90° (feathered)
  • Power: 0 kW

Understanding Active Stall Control

Our turbine uses "Active Stall" - let's understand this better.

What is "Stall"?

Airfoil stall occurs when:

  • Angle of attack becomes too large (>12-15° typically)
  • Airflow separates from upper surface
  • Becomes turbulent
  • Lift decreases dramatically
  • Drag increases

In wind turbines:

Passive Stall (old design):

  • Blade designed to stall naturally above rated wind
  • No pitch control needed
  • Simple, but uncontrollable

Active Pitch-to-Feather (most common):

  • Pitch increases to reduce angle of attack
  • Turns blade edge-toward wind
  • Smooth, controllable reduction in Cp
  • Requires large pitch range (0° to 90°)

Active Stall (our turbine):

  • Pitch DECREASES (negative pitch) to increase angle of attack
  • Forces blade to stall earlier than natural
  • Achieves power limiting with smaller pitch range
  • More responsive than passive stall
  • Less pitch travel than pitch-to-feather

Why Active Stall?

Advantages:

  • Smaller pitch actuators (less travel needed: -10° to +10° vs. 0° to 90°)
  • Faster pitch response
  • Lower actuator power consumption
  • Good control authority

Disadvantages:

  • Higher tower loads (more drag in stalled condition)
  • More complex control algorithms
  • Less common (harder to find expertise)

Our turbine's strategy in Region 4:

Actually uses combination approach:

  • Below ~15 m/s: Pitch-to-feather (pitch increases positively)
  • Above ~15 m/s: Active stall (pitch might go negative)
  • Blends both strategies for best results

This is why it's sometimes called "active pitch" rather than strictly "active stall"

Advanced Topic: Pitch Control Algorithms

How does the Controller decide what pitch to command?

Below Rated Wind (Regions 2 & 3):

Simple strategy:

IF wind_speed < 11 m/s THEN

    pitch = 0°  // Maximize Cp

END IF

Better strategy (what's actually used):

IF wind_speed < 11 m/s THEN

    IF power < 1,280 kW THEN

        pitch = optimize_for_TSR(wind_speed, blade_rpm)

    ELSE

        // Approaching rated, start limiting

        pitch = pitch + small_increase

    END IF

END IF

At/Above Rated Wind (Region 4):

Power-based control:

power_error = target_power - actual_power

pitch_adjustment = K_p * power_error + K_i * integral(power_error)


new_pitch = current_pitch + pitch_adjustment


// Limit pitch rate

IF (new_pitch - current_pitch) > max_rate THEN

    new_pitch = current_pitch + max_rate

END IF

This is a PI controller (Proportional-Integral):

Proportional term (K_p):

  • Responds to current error
  • Larger error → larger correction
  • Fast response

Integral term (K_i):

  • Responds to accumulated error
  • Eliminates steady-state error
  • Prevents offset

Example values:

  • Target power: 1,300 kW
  • Actual power: 1,350 kW
  • Error: -50 kW (too much power)
  • P term: K_p × (-50) = maybe -0.5° (decrease pitch)
  • I term: K_i × integral = maybe -0.2° (accumulated error)
  • Total: -0.7° pitch decrease
  • New pitch: 10° - 0.7° = 9.3°
  • Reduces power capture
  • Brings power back to target

Tuning the controller:

K_p too high:

  • Fast response
  • But oscillates
  • Unstable

K_p too low:

  • Slow response
  • Sluggish
  • Power overshoots

K_i too high:

  • Eliminates error quickly
  • But can cause overshoot
  • Hunting behavior

K_i too low:

  • Slow to eliminate offset
  • Persistent error
  • Sub-optimal

Typical values (site-specific):

  • K_p = 0.01 to 0.05
  • K_i = 0.001 to 0.01
  • Max pitch rate = 2-5 °/sec

Gain scheduling:

Actually, K_p and K_i vary with operating point:

IF wind_speed < 13 m/s THEN

    K_p = 0.02  // Moderate gain

ELSE IF wind_speed < 18 m/s THEN

    K_p = 0.04  // Higher gain (more aggressive)

ELSE

    K_p = 0.06  // Very high gain (near cutout)

END IF

Why varying gain?

  • Different sensitivity at different winds
  • Higher winds → need more aggressive control
  • Optimizes response across full range

Common Issues and Troubleshooting

Issue 1: "Power Oscillating at Rated Wind"

Symptom:

  • Wind steady at 12 m/s
  • Power oscillating: 1,250 → 1,350 → 1,250 → 1,350 kW
  • Pitch also oscillating: +5° → +8° → +5° → +8°
  • Period: ~5-10 seconds

Possible causes:

  • PI controller poorly tuned
  • K_p too high (over-responsive)
  • K_i causing integral windup
  • Mechanical resonance

Diagnosis:

  • Plot power and pitch vs. time
  • Check phase relationship
  • Is pitch leading or lagging power?
  • Calculate oscillation frequency

Solution:

  • Reduce K_p (less aggressive)
  • Reduce K_i (less integral action)
  • Add deadband (don't respond to tiny errors)
  • Filter wind measurement (reduce noise)

Proper tuning:

  • Should see small ripple (<±50 kW)
  • Not large swings
  • Smooth approach to target

Issue 2: "Power Below Target in High Wind"

Symptom:

  • Wind at 15 m/s
  • Should produce 1,300 kW
  • Actually producing 1,150 kW
  • Pitch seems normal (~+10°)

Possible causes:

  • Pitch actuator not reaching commanded angle
  • Mechanical limit reached
  • Blade aerodynamics degraded (dirt, ice, damage)
  • Power measurement error
  • Generator issue

Diagnosis:

  • Check: Commanded pitch vs. actual pitch
  • Are they the same?
  • If different: Actuator problem
  • If same: Aerodynamic or electrical problem

Example:

  • Commanded: +10°
  • Actual: +7° (actuator not reaching target)
  • Blade extracting more power than desired
  • But still less than generator capacity
  • Net result: Below rated power

Solution:

  • Service pitch actuator
  • Check hydraulic pressure (if hydraulic system)
  • Check motor/gearbox (if electric system)
  • Recalibrate position sensors

Issue 3: "Generator Overheating at Rated Power"

Symptom:

  • Wind at 12 m/s
  • Power at 1,300 kW (correct)
  • But generator temperature climbing
  • Exceeds 140°C (warning threshold)

Possible causes:

  • Cooling system degraded
  • Pitch not limiting effectively (brief overloads)
  • Harmonic currents (power quality issue)
  • Ambient temperature very high

Diagnosis:

  • Check cooling fan operation
  • Monitor power spikes (is it ever >1,300 kW?)
  • Check power factor, harmonics
  • Check ambient temperature

Solution:

  • Reduce rated power setpoint slightly (1,250 kW instead of 1,300 kW)
  • Gives temperature margin
  • Sacrifices 50 kW production
  • But prevents overheating shutdown
  • Better than frequent emergency stops!

Parameter change:

target_power = 1,250 kW  // Reduced from 1,300 kW

Issue 4: "Pitch Stuck at One Angle"

Symptom:

  • Pitch showing +15° constantly
  • Wind varying 10-20 m/s
  • Power varying wildly
  • Pitch not responding

Possible causes:

  • Pitch actuator seized
  • Hydraulic valve stuck
  • Position sensor failed
  • Control signal lost

This is SERIOUS FAULT!

Immediate action:

  • Emergency shutdown
  • Cannot control power without pitch
  • Unsafe to continue

Diagnosis:

  • Check pitch actuator status
  • Try manual pitch command
  • Does actuator move?
  • Check position feedback

Solution:

  • Requires maintenance
  • Cannot restart until pitch working
  • Might need actuator replacement
  • Costly repair: $10,000-50,000
  • Downtime: Days to weeks

Observations and Questions

Things to Notice:

1. Pitch is always active:

Even in steady wind:

  • Small continuous adjustments
  • Optimizing continuously
  • Never truly "steady"
  • This is normal!

2. Wind cubed relationship:

Power increases dramatically with wind:

  • 5 m/s → 240 kW
  • 10 m/s (2×) → 1,050 kW (4.4×)
  • 15 m/s (3×) → Would be 3,900 kW (16×) if not limited!

V³ relationship is powerful!

3. Most energy from mid-range winds:

Not from highest winds (limited to rated) Not from lowest winds (poor TSR) From 8-13 m/s range (good TSR + high power)

Site selection matters:

  • Site with steady 10 m/s average: Excellent
  • Site with variable 5-20 m/s: OK, but more limited
  • Site with steady 15 m/s: Good, but lots of limiting

4. Pitch travel vs. power control:

Small pitch changes (0° → +5°) control a lot:

  • At 13 m/s: +7° limits from 2,600 kW to 1,300 kW
  • 50% power reduction with just 7° pitch!

High sensitivity means:

  • Precise control possible
  • But also: Small errors matter
  • Calibration is critical

Scenario:

  • One blade damaged (rough surface)
  • Cp reduced by 10% for that blade

  • What would happen?

Answer:

  • Cannot compensate for one damaged blade
  • Power output would be reduced
  • Vibration would increase (imbalance)
  • Would need blade repair!

Q4: What if TSR could be optimized continuously?

Variable-speed turbine advantage:

  • Adjust RPM to maintain TSR = 7.5 always
  • At 5 m/s: Slow down to 13 RPM
  • At 15 m/s: Speed up to 29 RPM
  • Always maximum Cp!

Calculate improvement:

  • Fixed-speed annual energy: ~26,500 kWh/day
  • Variable-speed annual energy: ~29,000 kWh/day
  • Improvement: ~10%
  • This is why variable-speed dominates modern turbines!
