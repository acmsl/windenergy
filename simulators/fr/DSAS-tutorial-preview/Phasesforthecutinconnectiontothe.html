This section presents the result of the analysis made with real data from a Bonus B1300 Wind Turbine in production.

In a typical operation for the Wind Turbine to cutin (to reach the production state) the evolution of the rotor speed is as shown in the next figure. (Data obtained with ACM Datalogger 1300 on a Bonus 1300 Wind Turbine).



Soon after the brakes are released, the pitch takes a high value so that the intake of the wind energy through the Blades minimizes the time to arrive to cutin. The DriveTrain accelerates rapidly till certain acceleration rate is achieved. After that (blue region) the controller of the Wind Turbine modifies in real time the value of the pitch to accommodate for a constant acceleration.

Next figure shows, with a bigger time zoom, the behavior around cutin:



The blue region in this figure shows that there is a flow of current before reaching the synchronous speed (1500 rpms) due to the conduction of the thyristors. That current is taken by the generator to magnetize its windings.

Shortly after crossing the synchronous speed (yellow region) the thyristors are bypassed and there is a change of the reactive current (from inductive to capacitive) following the pattern of a asynchronous generator.

The pitch value, that was maintained with a negative values of the Cp ( see aerodynamic coefficient), ie. forcing the Blades to act as air brakes during the approach to cutin ( in order to minimize the risk of a run away ), increases its value, counteracting the fact that the flow of active power to the Grid acts as a mechanical brake that must be compensated for, taking more energy from the wind.
