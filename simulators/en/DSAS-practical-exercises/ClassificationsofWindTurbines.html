Classifications of Wind Turbines

What is a Wind Turbine? ››

Wind Turbine Classification by Blades Rotation Plane

The most common in industrial use (in Wind Turbines) is the Horizontal Axis Wind Turbine ( HAWT )

  • The blades rotate in a vertical plane
  • Around an axis kept (almost) horizontal
  • The wind flow, parallel to the ground, strikes perpendicular to the blade rotation plane

The axis, known as the "DriveTrain," rotates horizontally and is located on an elevated platform called the "Nacelle."

Location of the Blade Rotation Plane

The blade rotation plane must be kept perpendicular to the wind, and to the ground, but can be located :

  • Upwind (barlovento) - the blades face the incoming wind (what the wind hits first)
  • Downwind (sotavento) - behind the nacelle

The theoretical advantage of the downwind method is that the wind itself orients the nacelle. However, it has the disadvantage that there is wind losses when it hits the nacelle and tower before reaching the blades. Therefore, the vast majority of HAWT wind turbines are oriented facing the wind (upwind). This means that the Wind Turbine must have a mechanism to keep this plane "facing" the wind.

Classification by the number of Blades

Generally, they have three blades, although industrial models with two blades exist.

Classification by the type of Electric Converters

Regarding electric converters: the most common type being Asynchronous induction generator. An small percentage use Permanent magnet induction generator  

Among those with Asynchronous induction generator most of them do require Electronic Power Converters (see DFIG).

All wind turbines that uses Permanent magnet induction generator require Electronic Power Converters. 

Classification by the requirement of a Gearbox

When asynchronous induction generators are used, a gearbox is necessary because:

  • The DriveTrain typically rotates at speeds of 10 to 40 rpm
  • Asynchronous induction generators operate in the range of 1000 to 2000 rpm

When permanent magnet induction generators are used, some designs can work directly at the DriveTrain's rotation speed and therefore don't need a gearbox.
