What is a Wind Turbine?

What is a Wind Turbine?

A wind turbine is an electric power generation plant capable of transforming the kinetic energy of wind into electrical energy.

It can be described as two power converters working together:

  • The first transforms the wind's linear kinetic energy into rotational kinetic energy
  • The second transforms this rotational energy into electrical energy

First Converter: The "DriveTrain"

The first converter is called the "DriveTrain" and is the mechanical system that captures wind energy and converts it into rotational motion. It consists of:

Blades

  • These are the large airfoil-shaped structures that oppose the wind flow
  • When wind strikes the blades, it creates aerodynamic forces that make them want to move
  • The blades are designed with a special cross-sectional shape (airfoil) similar to airplane wings
  • This shape generates lift forces that cause rotation

Hub

  • The hub is the central component where all blades connect
  • It's mounted on a bearing system that allows only one degree of freedom: rotation around the horizontal axis
  • This means the hub and blades can only spin—they cannot move up, down, sideways, or tilt
  • By restricting movement to rotation only, all the wind's pushing force is converted into spinning motion

How it works together:

When wind strikes the blades, it tries to push them in various directions. However, because the hub only allows rotational movement, this pushing force is transformed into rotational energy. Think of it like a door that can only swing open and closed—no matter which direction you push it, it can only rotate around its hinges.

This rotating assembly (blades + hub + shaft) is what we call the DriveTrain, and it's the first step in converting wind's linear motion into the rotational motion needed to drive an electrical generator.

Second Converter: The Electrical Generator

The second converter is an electrical generator whose rotor is connected to the DriveTrain. In most cases, this connection is made through a gearbox because the optimal working conditions of the two converters—basically their shaft speeds—don't match.

Types of Electrical Generators

The electrical generator can be of several types. The most common are:

  • Asynchronous with squirrel cage
  • Asynchronous with rotor winding access
  • Synchronous
  • Synchronous with permanent magnets

Depending on the generator type, wind turbines have additional Power Electronics equipment for operation.

How Power is Controlled

The electrical generator makes the electrical power sent to the Grid appear as mechanical resistance on its shaft, opposing the DriveTrain's rotation.

In the type of wind turbine simulated here, the generator is a "squirrel cage" type with the stator winding directly connected to the Grid.

Wind Turbine Types by Axis Orientation

There are several types of wind turbines based on the axis position around which the blades rotate: vertical and horizontal. Those installed in Wind Farms are almost all horizontal axis types, known by the acronym HAWT (Horizontal Axis Wind Turbine).

These wind turbines have an orientation mechanism that keeps the blade rotation plane perpendicular to the wind direction.
