Practical Lab 2: Normal Shutdown Sequence (Cutout)

Objective:

Understand the complete shutdown sequence from production to stopped state. You'll learn:

  • How the Controller executes a controlled shutdown
  • The step-by-step sequence for safe disconnection
  • Differences between normal shutdown and emergency stop
  • What happens to each subsystem during cutout
  • How long the process takes
  • When and why the Controller initiates automatic shutdowns

Preparation

1. Starting State

You need the turbine running in production. If not already running from Lab 1:

  • Set wind speed: 8.0 m/s (good production wind)
  • Press START
  • Wait for complete startup sequence
  • Verify turbine in production:
    • State: "Production Large" (using 4-pole generator at this wind)
    • Power output: ~600-700 kW
    • Bypass contactors: Closed
    • Brakes: Released

2. Check Your Observation Points

Make sure you can see:

  • Control Terminal (main status)
  • State Machine displays
  • Power output graph
  • Blade pitch indicator
  • Generator speed
  • Incident log

3. Prepare to Record Timing

Have a way to note timestamps or durations:

  • When you press STOP
  • When power reaches zero
  • When disconnection occurs
  • When brake applies
  • When blades stop

You're ready to perform a controlled shutdown!

Procedure - The Normal Shutdown Sequence

Phase 1: Initiate Shutdown (Manual)

Step 1.1 - Press STOP

In the Control Terminal:

  • Locate the "STOP" button
  • Click it
  • Record the time

What happens immediately:

Incident created:

  • A new incident appears in "Open Incidents"
  • Description: "Manual STOP"
  • Start time: Current time
  • Recovery time: [empty] (won't recover until you clear it)
  • This incident MUST be acknowledged before turbine can restart

State Machine changes:

  • Operation State: "Production Large" → "Cutout in Progress"
  • Last Event: "EStop" (Stop button pressed event)

Step 1.2 - Observe Initial Response

The Controller begins the shutdown procedure, but nothing dramatic happens yet. It's planning the safe shutdown sequence.

Current status:

  • Still producing power
  • Still connected to grid
  • Bypass contactors still closed
  • Preparing to reduce power

Phase 2: Power Reduction

The first priority is to reduce power output smoothly to zero.

What's happening:

Pitch control strategy:

  • Gradually increase pitch angle
  • Turns blades more edge-on to wind
  • Reduces angle of attack
  • Decreases Cp (power coefficient)
  • Power extraction drops smoothly

Why gradual, not sudden?

  • Protects gearbox (no shock loads)
  • Smooth transition for grid
  • Less stress on pitch mechanism
  • Professional, controlled shutdown

Generator speed behavior:

  • Decreases slightly as power drops
  • Getting closer to synchronous speed (1500 RPM)
  • Less slip = less power
  • Approaching point where it can disconnect safely

Step 2.2 - Approaching Zero Power

When power drops below ~5 kW:

  • Generator speed very close to 1500 RPM (sync speed)
  • Almost no torque being transmitted
  • Nearly no electrical power flowing
  • Ready for disconnection

Record the time: How long to reduce power to nearly zero?

Phase 3: Grid Disconnection

Now the electrical disconnection happens.

Step 3.1 - Open Bypass Contactors

The first electrical disconnection:

What happens:

  1. Controller commands bypass contactors to OPEN
  2. Contactors open (typically < 0.5 seconds)
  3. Current stops flowing through contactors
  4. But thyristors are still in parallel!
  5. If any thyristor happens to be conducting, small current continues

Status indicators:

  • "Bypass Contactors: Open"
  • Power output: drops to 0 kW (or very close)
  • Generator still rotating (freewheeling)

Step 3.2 - Ensure Thyristors Off

Thyristor safety check:

What happens:

  1. Gate signals to thyristors already OFF
  2. Any residual conduction stops at next current zero-crossing
  3. Within 1/50th second (0.02 sec), all thyristors fully off
  4. Complete electrical isolation achieved

Step 3.3 - Generator Configuration Reset

The generator contactors open:

Which contactor was closed?

  • We were using Large Generator (4-pole)
  • So "Large Generator Contactor" was CLOSED

What happens:

  1. Controller commands Large Generator Contactor to OPEN
  2. Contactor opens (< 0.5 seconds)
  3. Generator winding now completely disconnected
  4. Generator is electrically isolated from grid

Status indicators:

  • "Large Generator Contactor: Open"
  • "Generator: Disconnected"

The generator is now freewheeling:

  • Still spinning (~1495 RPM and slowly decreasing)
  • But no electrical connection
  • No power generation
  • Kinetic energy in rotor gradually dissipating

Record the time: When did complete disconnection occur?

What's happening:

Aerodynamic braking:

  • At high pitch angles (>45°), Cp becomes negative
  • Blades actively slow the rotation
  • Energy extracted from rotation → wasted in air turbulence
  • This is intentional! We want to stop.

At 90° (feathered):

  • Blade edge-on to wind
  • Minimal force in rotation direction
  • Maximum aerodynamic braking
  • Blades act like air brakes

Step 4.2 - Apply Mechanical Brake

When should the mechanical brake be applied?

Controller logic:

The Controller waits for blade speed to drop sufficiently:

  • Target: Below ~8 RPM (for Large Generator case)
  • Or below ~5 RPM (for Small Generator case)

Why wait?

  • Applying brake at high speed creates shock
  • Wears brake pads excessively
  • Can damage brake disc
  • Better to use aerodynamic braking first
  • Mechanical brake just for final stopping

When speed is low enough:

What happens:

  1. Controller closes hydraulic brake valve
  2. Pressure releases from brake cylinder
  3. Spring force pushes brake pads against disc
  4. Friction stops rotation
  5. Takes 2-5 seconds to come to complete stop

Watch for:

Hydraulic Pressure:

  • Drops as valve releases pressure
  • Falls from ~85 bar to ~0 bar
  • Indicates brake applying

Brake Released Sensor:

  • Changes from "Active" → "Not Active"
  • Confirms pads are against disc

Rotor Speed:

  • Drops rapidly to zero
  • Final deceleration from ~6 RPM to 0 RPM
  • May see slight vibration/bouncing as stops

Generator Speed:

  • Drops to zero following rotor (via gearbox)
  • Complete stop

Record the time: When did rotation completely stop?

Phase 5: Final Securing

Step 5.1 - Verify Everything Stopped

Controller performs final checks:

Mechanical:

  • ✓ Rotor speed: 0 RPM
  • ✓ Generator speed: 0 RPM
  • ✓ Brake applied (sensor confirms)
  • ✓ Blade pitch: 90° (feathered)

Electrical:

  • ✓ All contactors open
  • ✓ No power flowing
  • ✓ Grid still available (for next start)

Step 5.2 - Update State Machine

Operation State Machine:

  • State: "Cutout in Progress" → "STOP"
  • Last Event: "ECutoutComplete"
  • Ready for next START command

Security State Machine:

  • State: "SS0BypassOn" → "SS0Idle"
  • No active alarms
  • Safe, idle condition

Step 5.3 - Final Status

The turbine is now fully stopped:

Status indicators:

  • State: "STOP"
  • Power: 0 kW
  • Blade speed: 0 RPM
  • Generator speed: 0 RPM
  • Pitch: 90° (feathered)
  • Brake: Applied
  • All contactors: Open
  • Generator: Disconnected

Open Incidents:

  • "Manual STOP" (requires acknowledgment before restart)

Analysis - Understanding the Shutdown

Total Shutdown Time Breakdown:

Typical timing for normal cutout:

  1. Power reduction phase: 25-35 seconds
  2. Electrical disconnection: 1-2 seconds
  3. Aerodynamic braking: 20-30 seconds
  4. Mechanical braking: 2-5 seconds

Total: 50-75 seconds typically

Much faster than startup (2-4 minutes), but still controlled and safe.

Why This Sequence?

Each phase has a purpose:

Phase 2 - Power reduction first:

  • Never disconnect under load!
  • Reduces electrical stress
  • Prevents arc in contactors
  • Grid-friendly (smooth reduction)
  • Allows time to verify everything working

Phase 3 - Electrical before mechanical:

  • Disconnect electrically first
  • Then stop mechanically
  • If reversed: trying to stop while generating
  • Creates huge electrical stress
  • Could damage generator

Phase 4 - Aerodynamic before mechanical brake:

  • Free aerodynamic braking (no wear)
  • Reduces brake pad wear
  • Less shock to drivetrain
  • Mechanical brake just for final stopping

Safety Features Observed:

Multiple confirmations:

  • Power verified at zero before disconnect
  • Contactors verified open
  • Speed verified low before brake
  • Brake application verified by sensor

Graceful degradation:

  • If power doesn't reduce → don't disconnect
  • If aerodynamic brake insufficient → mechanical brake compensates
  • System prioritizes safety at each step

The "Manual STOP" Incident:

This is important to understand:

Why create an incident?

  • Forces operator awareness
  • Prevents accidental restart
  • Creates audit trail (who stopped it? when?)
  • Industry best practice

Must be acknowledged:

  • Operator must click "OK" checkbox
  • Confirms: "I know this turbine was stopped"
  • Confirms: "I'm ready to allow restart"
  • Prevents automation from restarting during maintenance

Recovery time:

  • Stays empty (doesn't self-recover)
  • Unlike faults that recover automatically
  • Operator intervention required

Comparison: Normal Stop vs. Emergency Stop

Let's understand the differences:

Normal Stop (What You Just Did):

Characteristics:

  • Gradual power reduction (30+ seconds)
  • Gentle electrical disconnection
  • Aerodynamic braking first
  • Mechanical brake only at low speed
  • Total time: ~60 seconds
  • Minimizes component wear

When used:

  • Manual operator stop
  • Planned maintenance
  • Wind out of range (too low or too high)
  • Minor non-critical faults
  • End of production period

Emergency Stop:

Characteristics:

  • Immediate power reduction (5-10 seconds)
  • Rapid electrical disconnection
  • May skip aerodynamic braking
  • Mechanical brake applied quickly, even at higher speed
  • Total time: ~10-20 seconds
  • Harder on equipment, but necessary

When used:

  • Overspeed detected
  • Major component failure
  • Safety system activation
  • Grid fault (phase loss, over/under voltage)
  • Tower vibration excessive
  • Emergency stop button pressed

Observations and Questions

Things to Notice:

1. Power reduction curve:

  • Not linear!
  • Faster at high power (larger pitch changes)
  • Slower near zero (fine control)
  • Why? Cp vs. pitch is non-linear

2. Generator speed during shutdown:

  • Decreases gradually
  • Approaches sync speed (1500 RPM)
  • At sync: zero slip, zero power
  • Below sync: would become motor (we disconnect first!)

3. Pitch behavior:

  • Continuous smooth motion
  • No sudden jumps
  • Hydraulic system (or electric) controls this smoothly
  • Takes time, but gentle on mechanism

4. Brake timing:

  • Why wait until ~6 RPM?
  • Less shock, less wear
  • But not too low (don't wait forever)
  • Balance between time and stress

Q4: What happens to temperatures during shutdown?

Automatic Shutdowns (Controller-Initiated)

Not all shutdowns are manual! The Controller initiates automatic shutdowns for various reasons.

Automatic Shutdown Triggers:

1. Wind Speed Out of Range

Too high (> 25 m/s):

  • Wind speed exceeds safe operating limit
  • Controller initiates cutout
  • Creates incident: "Wind Speed Too High for Operation"
  • Stays stopped until wind decreases below 23 m/s (hysteresis)
  • Requires acknowledgment

Too low (< 3.3 m/s):

  • Not enough wind to maintain production
  • Power output dropping
  • Better to shutdown than run inefficiently
  • Creates incident: "Wind Speed Too Low for Operation"
  • Will auto-restart when wind increases above 3.5 m/s
  • Usually doesn't require acknowledgment (unless configured to)

2. Grid Fault

Phase loss:

  • One phase voltage drops to zero
  • Immediate cutout (emergency-like, faster)
  • Creates incident: "Grid Phase [X] Lost"
  • Requires acknowledgment
  • Won't restart until all phases present

Over/Under voltage:

  • Voltage outside acceptable range (typically 620-760V)
  • Brief delay (2-5 seconds) to see if recovers
  • If persists: cutout
  • Creates incident: "Grid Voltage Fault"

Frequency deviation:

  • Grid frequency outside 49.5-50.5 Hz (or 59.5-60.5 Hz for 60Hz grids)
  • Indicates grid instability
  • Cutout to protect equipment

3. Temperature Faults

Generator overheating:

  • Winding temperature > 140°C (warning)

155°C (alarm, cutout)

  • Possible causes:
    • Cooling fan failure
    • Blocked air intake
    • Overload
  • Creates incident: "Generator Temperature Excessive"

Gearbox overheating:

  • Oil temperature > 85°C (warning)

95°C (cutout)

  • Possible causes:
    • Cooling system failure
    • Low oil level
    • Bearing failure
  • Creates incident: "Gearbox Temperature Excessive"

4. Overspeed Protection

VCU (Velocity Centrifugal Unit) activation:

  • Generator speed > 1803 RPM
  • Mechanical overspeed detector on generator shaft
  • Triggers immediate emergency shutdown
  • Very serious fault
  • Creates incident: "Overspeed Detected - VCU"

HCU (High-speed Centrifugal Unit) activation:

  • Blade speed > 26 RPM
  • Backup overspeed detector on rotor shaft
  • Triggers if VCU failed
  • Creates incident: "Overspeed Detected - HCU"

5. Vibration

Tower vibration excessive:

  • Accelerometer detects unusual vibration
  • Could indicate:
    • Blade damage/imbalance
    • Bearing failure
    • Foundation problem
  • Shutdown to prevent damage
  • Creates incident: "Excessive Vibration Detected"

6. Control System Faults

Pitch system failure:

  • Pitch doesn't respond to commands
  • Difference between commanded and actual > 5° for > 10 sec
  • Can't control power → unsafe
  • Shutdown required

Brake system failure:

  • Can't verify brake release
  • Can't verify brake application
  • Safety critical system
  • Shutdown and investigate

Sensor failures:

  • Critical sensors (anemometer, speed sensors, etc.)
  • If can't measure, can't control safely
  • Shutdown until sensors repaired/replaced

Try This: Automatic Shutdown Due to High Wind

Let's trigger an automatic cutout:

Setup:

  1. Have turbine running normally (8 m/s, production)
  2. Monitor Control Terminal

Execute:

  1. Gradually increase wind speed from 8 to 26 m/s
  2. Watch what happens as you approach 25 m/s

What you'll observe:

At 25 m/s (threshold exceeded):

  • Incident created: "Wind Speed Excessive"
  • Controller initiates automatic cutout
  • Same sequence as manual stop!
  • Power reduction → disconnection → braking → stop

After shutdown:

  • State: "STOP"
  • Incident in "Open Incidents" (requires acknowledgment)
  • Won't restart even if you press START
  • Must acknowledge incident first

Lower wind speed:

  • Reduce wind to 10 m/s
  • Acknowledge the incident
  • Press START
  • Should restart normally

This is normal, safe operation!

The turbine is protecting itself from dangerous wind conditions.

Try This: Automatic Shutdown Due to Low Wind

Setup:

  1. Have turbine running (7 m/s, production)

Execute:

  1. Gradually decrease wind speed from 7 to 3 m/s
  2. Watch what happens as you approach 3.3 m/s

What you'll observe:

At 3.3 m/s (threshold):

  • Power dropping
  • Becoming uneconomical to run
  • Controller waits a bit (maybe 30 seconds)
  • Confirms wind staying low
  • Initiates cutout

Incident created:

  • "Wind Speed Too Low"
  • Recovery time: [empty] (waiting for wind to increase)

Behavior difference:

  • May not require acknowledgment (depends on configuration)
  • Often configured for auto-restart
  • When wind increases above 3.5 m/s:
    • Incident auto-recovers
    • Automatic restart sequence begins
    • No operator intervention needed

This is common and expected!

Wind is variable - turbines stop and start many times per day in variable wind conditions.

Common Issues and Troubleshooting

Issue 1: "Shutdown Taking Too Long"

Symptom:

  • Pressed STOP several minutes ago
  • Power reduced to near zero
  • But turbine hasn't disconnected
  • State stuck in "Cutout in Progress"

Possible causes:

  • Waiting for power to reach exactly zero (patience!)
  • Pitch system not responding (power won't drop)
  • Controller waiting for some condition

Diagnosis:

  • Check current power output (is it still dropping?)
  • Check blade pitch (is it increasing?)
  • Check for any new incidents/errors

Solution:

  • Usually: just wait (shutdown takes time)
  • If pitch not moving: pitch system fault
  • May need emergency stop (we'll cover in Lab 5)

Issue 2: "Brake Won't Apply"

Symptom:

  • Shutdown completed electrically
  • Blades still spinning slowly
  • Brake should have applied by now
  • State waiting for complete stop

Possible causes:

  • Hydraulic valve stuck
  • Brake release sensor stuck/failed
  • Hydraulic pressure not releasing

Diagnosis:

  • Check hydraulic pressure (should drop to near zero)
  • Check brake sensor status
  • Check for hydraulic system faults

Solution:

  • In real turbine: serious fault, needs maintenance
  • In simulator: may be injected fault (for training)

Issue 3: "Can't Restart After Shutdown"

Symptom:

  • Performed normal shutdown
  • Wind conditions good
  • Press START
  • Nothing happens or error message

Possible causes:

  • Forgot to acknowledge "Manual STOP" incident!
  • Other unacknowledged incidents
  • Grid fault occurred during shutdown
  • Wind actually not in range

Diagnosis:

  • Check "Open Incidents" tab
  • Any unacknowledged incidents? → Acknowledge them
  • Check grid status (all phases present?)
  • Check actual wind speed

Solution:

  • Acknowledge all open incidents
  • Verify all pre-start conditions met
  • Try START again

Issue 4: "Shutdown Aborted / Restarted Automatically"

Symptom:

  • Started manual shutdown
  • Partway through, shutdown stopped
  • Turbine restarting automatically

Possible causes:

  • Pressed START during shutdown (cancelled it)
  • Auto-restart configured for certain conditions
  • Unusual but possible in some control configurations

Diagnosis:

  • Check incident log for events
  • What state is Operation State Machine in?
  • Any new "Start" events logged?

Solution:

  • If you want it stopped: press STOP again
  • If auto-restart unwanted: investigate controller settings
  • Usually this doesn't happen (shutdown completes once initiated)
