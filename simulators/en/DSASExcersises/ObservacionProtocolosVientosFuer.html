🎯 Protocols for strong winds

Objective: To understand the automatic protocols that are activated in strong winds.

What to observe: Wind speed, status changes, power limitation, or shutdown.

What to note: Protection sequence and final system status.

💡 Difference between progressive power reduction and complete shutdown.

Observation of behavior in very strong winds: several cases

Practical classes ›› Behavior in very strong winds ››

In this practical session, you should observe the protocols used by the Controller in relation to high winds.

1. Ensure that the wind turbine is disconnected from the grid. Press "STOP" if necessary. Ensure that there are no pending incidents, except for "Manual STOP."

2. Set a high wind speed, but lower than "Max for start-up" (18 m/s). Check that the wind turbine performs the grid connection operation (cutin) normally.

3. Disconnect the wind turbine from the grid: press "STOP."

4. Set the wind speed to above 18 m/s and check that the wind turbine performs the wind orientation operation but does not complete the connection to the grid. You can see that the controller is continuously monitoring the wind value: click on the "Messages" tab in the "Terminal Control" synoptic.

5. Reduce the wind speed to just below 18 m/s, for example 17.9 m/s. Check that the grid connection process (cutin) starts immediately.


Protocols for very high winds.

1. Connect the wind turbine to the grid. 

2. Slowly increase the wind speed using the arrows or page keys up to 25 m/s. ("Max for continuous operation" value.) Check that the wind turbine remains in production indefinitely.

3. Increase the wind speed above 25 m/s. You will see that after a certain amount of time, the controller disconnects the generator from the grid (cutout) and generates an incident with the message "Wind > WinMaxForOperation for more than 100 s."

4. If you now slowly decrease the wind, check that nothing happens until the wind is below 18 m/s.

5. Note that when the wind drops below 18 m/s, the incident is automatically moved to the "Incident history," and the Controller automatically initiates the process of connecting to the grid.

6. Slowly increase the wind speed using the arrows or page keys to a value slightly above 28 m/s, for example 28.1 m/s. (Value of "Max for temporary operation".) You will see that the Controller will initiate disconnection from the Grid (cutout) after a time that is significantly shorter than in point 3. Two incidents
will appear with the descriptions: "Wind > WindMaxForOperation for more than 100 s" and "Wind > WindMaxForTemporaryOperation for more than 5 s." Note that the second one has a slightly earlier "Start" time.

7. If the wind now slowly decreases, check that nothing happens until the wind speed is below 18 m/s.

8. Note that when the wind drops below 18 m/s, the incidents are automatically moved to the "Incident History," and the Controller automatically initiates the process of connecting to the Network.

9. Now increase the wind speed to 30 m/s ("Absolute Max" value). You will see that an incident with the description "Wind > WindAbsolutMax for more than 1 s" will be generated in a very short time, followed by two others similar to those in point 6.
