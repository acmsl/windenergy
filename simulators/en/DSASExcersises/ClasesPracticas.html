👋 Welcome to the ACMSL Virtual Wind Turbine Laboratory

With the practical exercises suggested in this document, you will become familiar with the behavior of the subsystems and common elements of wind turbines known as horizontal axis turbines, as well as with the specific characteristics of the family known as squirrel cage generators and aerodynamic loss input control.

You should be familiar with the description of this family (see Tutorial) and the execution environment of the Real-Time Simulators (see LdP).

🎯 Overall objectives of the module

  • Understand the general operation of a horizontal axis wind turbine.
  • Correctly interpret system states, alarms, and transitions.
  • Relate physical variables (wind, speed, power) to control behavior.
  • Identify protection strategies in the event of abnormal conditions.

⚠️ What you should know before you begin

  • Basic concepts of wind energy and aerodynamics.
  • Basic notions of three-phase electrical systems.
  • Basic principles of control and state machines.

📚 Skills you will develop

  • Ability to perform evidence-based technical analysis.
  • Preliminary diagnosis of subsystem failures.
  • Functional interpretation of state machines.
  • Understanding of the dynamic behavior of wind turbines.

📝 Recommended working methodology

  1. Read the objective of the exercise and identify the main variable (wind, speed, power, pressure, states).
  2. Observe at rest: note the initial state and reference values (before causing changes).
  3. Make a single change (one at a time) and record what happens.
  4. Look for evidence: states, alarms, transitions, trends, and times.
  5. Conclude: explain the cause and effect with 2–3 pieces of evidence (not just opinions).

✅ Observation checklist

  • States: What state is the system in? Does it change? Why?
  • Alarms: do any appear? Which was the first?
  • Key variables: wind, speed, power, direction, pressure (depending on the practice).
  • Transitions: moment of change and condition that triggers it (threshold, permission, time).
  • Final state: Is it stable? Is it blocked? Does it need to be reset?

📋 How to document your conclusion

Use this short format (4 lines):

  • Situation: what you were observing.
  • Event: what changed (and when).
  • Evidence: 2–3 measurable data points (variables/states/alarms).
  • Conclusion: cause-and-effect explanation (one clear sentence).

⚠️ Typical mistakes (to avoid)

  • Changing several parameters at once and not being able to identify the cause.
  • Concluding based only on an impression, without evidence (variables/statuses).
  • Confusing limitation with stoppage in high wind scenarios.
  • Ignoring the initial state (without reference, there is no comparison).

Practical classes

The following chapters present a set of exercises that will allow you to learn about the behavior of the wind turbine in different situations.

The list of these practical classes is not exhaustive, as it is possible to develop others that propose real problems in the wind turbine for the student to solve. Generally, these cases involve the development of 'configurations' different from those used in this simulator, although these differences do not refer to the core of the configuration, but rather to sets of parameters, connections between signals, etc. Please consult ACM SL for guidelines on developing these configurations.
