🎯 Automatic orientation (Yaw)

Objective: To analyze how the system automatically adjusts its orientation to align with the wind.

What to observe: Wind direction, alignment error, and yaw performance.

What to note: Minimum correction threshold and time required to achieve alignment.

💡 Identify whether the correction is continuous or in discrete steps.

Automatic Orientation

Practical classes ›› Effects of changes in wind direction ››

This practical exercise allows you to observe the wind values "Min for orientation" and "Min for start-up (cutin)" described in Functional wind values. 

The controller has a parameter, "Min for start-up," which is the minimum wind speed at which it is worthwhile to put the wind turbine into production.

There is another parameter, "Min for orientation," to implement the concept of "the wind is too low to start production but may increase: let's turn the nacelle into the wind to minimize start-up time."

1. Ensure that there are no open incidents and that the wind turbine is disconnected from the grid (press STOP to make sure).

2. Reduce the wind to 1 m/s using the "Page forward" and/or "Down arrow" keys.

3. Fill in the "Ok" column of the "Manual STOP" incident and press "START."

4. Change the wind direction so that the nacelle is very disoriented.

5. Slowly increase the wind value using the "Up Arrow" key. Check that the Controller does not give the command to orientate until the wind reaches 1.5 m/s.

6. Check that with this wind speed of 1.5 m/s the Gondola manages to orient itself, but does not start the grid connection operation (cutin).

7. Slowly increase the wind value using the "Up Arrow" key until approximately 3.6 m/s, at which point the grid connection process (cutin) begins.
