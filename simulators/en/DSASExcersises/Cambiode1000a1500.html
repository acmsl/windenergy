🎯 Change from 1000 to 1500

Objective: Observe the mode change associated with the jump from 1000 to 1500 and its impact on control.

What to observe: Target speed, control status, and power response.

What to note: Variable that triggers the change, before/after status, and transients.

💡 Note if power spikes/drops occur during the transition.

❓ Guiding questions

  1. How long does it take to change the generator/mode from low wind (< 6.2 m/s) to high wind (> 7 m/s)?
  2. What is the status of the system immediately after the change?

While in production, wind modification to change the connection from Small to Large Generator

Practical classes ››

The objective of this practical is to familiarize students with the concept of changing the "connection mode" of the electric generator to the grid, and the reasons why this functionality is necessary.

In this class, we assume that the wind turbine is connected to the grid (producing), with wind forcing the use of the generator's 6-pole connection (small generator). See Setting the conditions for entry into production with low wind.

In this experiment, we will increase the wind speed so that there is a change in the generator connection to 4 poles (large generator): this wind speed must be higher than the "Min for Large Gen." See Functional wind values. The minimum wind speed for this transition to occur is 6.6 m/s. Modify the wind speed to, for example, 12 m/s using the "Page Forward" keys (see step 7 in Setting the conditions for entry into production with low wind).

On a recorder with the "Cutin + Cutout + Production" panel, you will see the following signal evolution.

You can see that as the wind increases, the power generated increases: this occurs until the wind exceeds (for a certain time at least) the speed of 6.6 m/s. When this value is exceeded, the controller initiates an operation to change the generator connection, which will switch from a 6-pole connection (small generator) to a 4-pole connection (large generator). 

To do this, it must disconnect the generator from the grid: to do this, it changes the pitch of the blades so that the power generated is reduced and deactivates the thyristor bypass contactor. At that moment, the speed of rotation of the generator shaft has fallen below the synchronous speed of the "small generator," which is 1,000 rpm. In this situation, with the generator disconnected, the generator connection mode is changed. This is done using the contactors shown in the "Electrical diagram" tab of the "Electrical cabinet" synoptic. See the following figure.

At that moment, the controller begins a grid connection operation (cutin): it controls the pitch so that mechanical torque is extracted from the wind, which accelerates the power train; when the generator's rotational speed approaches the synchronous speed (now 1,500 rpm), the thyristors are progressively opened (see Soft starter (soft connection) to the grid), culminating in the activation of the thyristor bypass contactors.

A notable feature of the simulated wind turbine is that it is capable of performing this operation in a very short time, on the order of 25 seconds. This is important because of the loss of production that it entails.
