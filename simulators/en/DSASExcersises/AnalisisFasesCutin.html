🎯 Analysis of phases in Cut-in

Objective: Break down the start-up (cut-in) into phases and understand what condition triggers each transition.

What to observe: Machine states, permissions, and evolution of variables during startup.

What to note: Sequence of states, duration of each phase, and value that triggers the change.

💡 Record the exact moment of each transition (previous state → next state).

❓ Guiding questions

  1. How long does the controller take to verify that the hydraulic brake system is working?
  2. Approximately how long are the thyristors in conduction (partial or total)?

Detailed analysis of the phases of connection to the grid (Cutin)

Practical classes ››

This practical uses real data from a wind turbine like the simulated one, taken with a sampling rate of 1 sample per second.

Fig. 1 below shows a time zoom corresponding to the connection of the generator to the grid. Fig. 2 shows an enlarged detail with the interval immediately before and after the connection of the generator to the grid.

The objective is to observe how key variables evolve. In particular, observe the sudden change in the acceleration of the power train in this type of wind turbine, and in the main electrical variables.  


Fig. 1.

The blue area shows how the controller modifies the pitch value so that the acceleration of the power train ("RPM GENERATOR" signal) remains constant until connection is reached. The acceleration in this area is of the order of 23 rpm/second (= (1500 rpm - 800 rpm)/30 seconds). When production begins, this acceleration is practically 0 rpm/second: this occurs because, in production, the power extracted from the wind is equal to the (electrical) power delivered to the grid. These values give an idea of the mechanical stress to which the power train components and the tower are subjected at the moment of connection to the grid.

Just when the generator is connected (first in a limited way by the thyristors and then by the activation of the thyristor bypass contactors), there is a (significant) peak in "inductive" reactive power consumption (signal "PAveInd"). This power corresponds to the current flowing through the generator stator in the initial moments, to constitute the magnetic flux necessary for the "induction machine" to exist.

Immediately afterwards, a "capacitive" reactive power peak occurs (signal "PAveCap"), characteristic of the slip adjustment, from the synchronism passage (slip = 0) to the final value, corresponding to the mechanical power that forces the shaft. See Introduction to induction machines. Note that the initial value of this power is significant in relation to the active power (PAveAct signal) that is ultimately delivered to the grid.
