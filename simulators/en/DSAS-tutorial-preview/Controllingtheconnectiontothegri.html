Controlling the connection to the grid

Description of the Simulator ›› Emulated Wind Turbine ›› Controller ›› Algorithms ››

All electric power suppliers that can be connected to a Grid must has a behavior that should make minimum disturbance when connecting to and/or disconnecting from it.

The problem to solve is this: we have an electrical generator which starts the process disconnected and ends connected to the Grid. At the end , the rpms on the generator will be a little above its synchronous speed (i.e, 1.05). Before connection, the generator may be stopped. 

Different Wind Turbines has different solutions for this problem: for example the "Doubly Fed Induction Generator" Wind Turbines use their highly flexible and controllable Power Electronics Converters to provide very smooth  connections and disconnections.

Wind Turbines that have squirrel cage induction generators directly connected to the Grid, like the one used in the simulated Wind Turbine, use a "soft-starter" device. The procedure implemented by this hardware is based on the following ideas:

 


1.First accelerate the Generator shaft so its rpms reach near, but below, synchronous speed .

2.Use a Thyristor as intermediate controlled switch between the Generator and the Grid.

The soft connection of a squirrel cage generator directly connected to the Grid is done using Thyristors, following the sequence:

1.The Thyristor controller continuously monitors the Generator's rpms as soon as it approaches the synchronous speed.

2.The Controller of the DriveTrain controls its acceleration so it does not overcome a given limit. that does not get When the rpms reach that speed the start the conduction of the thyristors during a small part of the cycle.

3.At that moment the DriveTrain (wind forcing the Hub + gearbox + generator turning) now has a little new mechanical load coming from the current (small) flowing through the generator. This current is the magnetizing current needed to create the magnetic field inside the generator that will act as link between the mechanical power from the DriveTrain to Grid. Therefore the DriveTrain slows down a little bit.

4.In the following seconds (3 to 5) the gate control of the thyristors advance the moment of firing, so the their conduction angle increases. In that phase the generator is forced to adapt its speed to the Grid frequency: this is because the Grid is (except in island operation) much more powerful that the wind + DriveTrain. 

5.Finally the situation evolves so that the rotor of the generator is not only rotating with the same (corresponding) frequency as the Grid, but also in phase with it.

6.After that happens the voltage drop in the thyristors is very low (some volts at most) so the switches that bypass them can be close with no harm. The thyristors are no longer fired and the generator is directly connected to the Grid.
