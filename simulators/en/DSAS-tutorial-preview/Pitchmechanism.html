Brakes

The Brake system in a Wind Turbine has the following duties:

•To prevent the movement of the DriveTrain while in the "parking" position, typically during maintenance operations.

•To prevent over-speed in the DriveTrain. The Controller must be monitoring the DriveTrain rpms, and modifying the Pitch and/or the electrical load in order to keep them within safe limits. In case the upper limit is exceeded, the Brake system must stopped it.

Stopping the DriveTrain implies the dissipation of the energy stored on it; therefore, the use of the Brake system to stop the DriveTrain may result in a heavy deformation of the disc and/or caliper, depending on the DriveTrain rpms at that moment: i.e, a failure of the Wind Turbine, with direct cost of maintenance.

There are two places where the disc Brake can be placed: in the low speed axis (Rotor side) or ant the high speed axis (Generator side).  

Low-speed-shaft braking is relatively straightforward in that a large disc brake, with a large friction lining area, is easy to accommodate. The drawback is that the brake must generate a high-braking torque.

Generally, the most cost-effective position is on the high-speed shaft between a gearbox and generator. The increase ratios of wind-turbine gearboxes produce a large reduction in output torque.  The brake-pad area must be sufficient to control the temperature rise.

A further consideration regarding brake position is the possibility of gear tooth damage. If brakes are installed on the gearbox-output shaft they may require the use of forced lubrication between the mating teeth, in order to minimize the possibilities of fretting or gear damage.
