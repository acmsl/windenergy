What is a Wind Turbine

A Wind Turbine is a device that transforms (part of) the energy of the wind incident on it to electric energy.


To achieve that goal, the wind energy is first transformed in mechanical energy through Blades mounted on a Hub, and connected to an electric generator, directly or through a Gearbox and a Rotor Shaft. There are several types of electric generators  used in actual Wind Turbines: asynchronous generators, synchronous generators, synchronous generators with permanent magnets, etc.

The name "DriveTrain" is associated with the set of the Hub, Blades, Rotor Shaft, and the Gearbox.

There are many kinds of Wind Turbines, the main concepts for its classification are:

•the position of the rotating axis (horizontal or vertical).

•if the Pitch angel of the Blades is fixed or can be changed

•if the blades rotation plane is located upwind or downwind

•how the blades are used to counteract high wind (Stalling or "To the Feathers")

•if the DriveTrain Wind Turbine protects itself from high winds (stalling), etc.

This "Dual Speed Active Stall Wind Turbine Simulator", or DSAS Wind Turbine Simulator, is designed after a Wind Turbine model that uses an horizontal axis, three blades, with dual fixed DriveTrain speeds, with an asynchronous induction generator with short circuited rotor (squirrel cage) directly connected to the Grid, with DriveTrain control by active stall.
