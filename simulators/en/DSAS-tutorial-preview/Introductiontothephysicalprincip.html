A brief explanation on how the Induction Machine works is the following.

•First, recall that the Lenz's law for magnetic circuits says that a magnetic field (created for example by a magnet) interacts with a electric circuit if the electric circuit is a closed conducting loop traversed by this magnetic field. The interaction is as follows:

oIf there are changes in the magnetic field traversing the electric circuit, then an current is forced in the electric circuit.


•Second, we can change the magnetic field that traverses an electric circuit in a number of ways:

oWe can substitute the magnet by a coil supplied by a alternating frequency current.

oOr we can move the magnet, or the coil, or both.

oOr we can modify the physical form of the electric circuit.

Let's look at the magnetic fields created in the armature of the induction machine by the stator coils. Next figure shows an armature with a three-phase stator and one pole (ie. one coil per phase)


The important concept is that the interaction is bidirectional: i.e if we rotate the magnet then there will be currents induced in the stator windings. If we remove the magnet and apply a three-phase voltage (one per phase, separated by 120º) then  we will generate a rotating magnetic field similar to the generated by the physical magnet rotating. Thus, we can take mechanical power (rotating the shaft rotor manually) and convert it to electric power (first case: Generator) or take current from the Grid and create a magnetic field that will create mechanical torque, hence, mechanical power.
