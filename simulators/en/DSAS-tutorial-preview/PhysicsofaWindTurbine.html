Physics of a Wind Turbine

We provide here the basic principles to understand the behavior of big Wind Turbines connected to the Grid. There is a lot of bibliography on this subject.


One thing to point out is that although Wind Turbines and airplanes share the same aerodynamics principles, their use is very different: in an airplane the wind (in this case the wind relative in the wings, ie, the derived from the movement of the plane) serves to lift it, and ideally, the airplane wants to keep the displacement of the air particles to a minimum, because that means a stopping force, and therefore some resistance so waste of energy. On the contrary, the purpose of the blades in a Wind Turbine is stop the wind in order get energy from it. The Wind Turbine blades has things in common with propellers in helicopters.


There are several types of Wind Turbines in the market. The most common ones has a number of Blades (with one -seldom, two or three -most common) placed on a horizontal axis rotor. Attached to this rotor there is a electric generator. Depending on the model, that generator may be connected directly to the Grid or it can be the source for a DC power that supply to a power inverter, which is the element connected to the Grid.


The generator type are asynchronous with squirrel cage, asynchronous with doubly fed rotor, synchronous with permanent magnets, etc.


Depending on the type of generator the connection of with the rotor includes a gearbox. Typical speeds in the rotor are in the 10 to 30 rpms. Typical  speed for the generator range in th 1000 to 2000 rpms. The gearbox speed relation between axis is in the 1:50 to 1:90.


If the Blades has pitch control (pitch is equivalent to the angle of attack of a airplane wing), the mechanical actuators are located in a "Hub".


Controlling the pitch angle of the Blades is a tool to adapt to changes in the wind speed.


In order to use the maximum energy from the wind, the Blades are held facing it. This can be achieve either "upwind" or "downwind" respect to the wind direction. 


In short, the main characteristics that define a type of Wind Turbine are:


•Position of the axis where the Blades turn; horizontal or vertical

•Number and form of Blades.

•If the blade's pitch angle can be changed.

•If the rotor speed in production can change.

•If the generator is connected directly to the Grid.

•If there are power electronic converters.

•How the is done the control of the production: fixed Blades with stalling, variable pitch ( direct or active stalling) (see next page) 


The Wind Turbine model we use in this simulator has the following characteristics: horizontal axis 3 Blades upwind, asynchronous generator (squirrel cage) directly connected to the Grid, with two ways of connection: 4 and 6 magnetic poles.
