Why the Hub turns

Physics of a Wind Turbine ›› Basic concepts ››

The basic principles governing the forces generated on the Wind Turbine Blades are the same forces that govern the lifting of an aircraft. The Blades rotate for the simple reason that they are mounted on a "device" (Hub) having only one degree of freedom: rotation in a plane perpendicular to the axis of the Hub.

Which are those forces?

Let's assume we have a blade in an wind tunnel: a place where we can create a controlled, uniform air flow, that does not create turbulences (i.e. "laminar flow"). Inside this wind tunnel we place a blade (delimited by the red line), and we  tightly attach it to the ground, so it can not move.  Let's assume that this is done with an internal bar that cross the blade along its longest axis, represented by the small circle in the next figure.

The effect of the wind coming from the left is a compression of the laminar flow in the bottom side of the blade and a depletion of this flow on its upper side. The result is a set of forces applied to every small piece of surface of the blade: this set of forces is indicated by the small dotted arrows. As the blade is a rigid body, we can measure the total effect of these forces by measuring the force that must support the supporting bar: this force is indicated by the red vector. The strength and direction of this force depends on the form of the blade, the incoming wind speed and the angle between the chord of the blade and the wind direction. We can decompose this red vector at will: but for our purposes it is convenient to think of this red vector as the addition of two forces: one going up, named Flifting, and another going to the right, named Fdragging.(Green vectors with origin in the central circle.)

If we change our scenario, and think of the figure above as the case of the wing of an airplane, with the still air, and the aircraft moving to the left , then,  the two forces, Fdragging and Flifting, explain why  the plane is hanging in the air (because of Flifting), and why it must push left (to overcome Fdragging).

Now let's look at the same blade, but placed at the wind turbine's Hub. The position is similar but inverted. See next figure.

In this case, the wind that is incident on the Blade edge is the "Total wind" (red vector), a composition of the actual wind (blue vector) and the Rotational wind (black vector) that the edge "sees" because it is rotating. The total force corresponding to the red vector in the former figure is represented by the green vector in the figure to the left. This force can be decomposed in two forces: 

a) The purple force, pulls the blade along the only degree of freedom (the rotational plane), and therefore force the DriveTrain to rotate.

b) The light brown vector goes in the direction of the Nacelle axis and is counteracted by the Tower. i.e. this is the force that bends the Tower.
