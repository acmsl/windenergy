Structure of a Wind Turbine

What is a Wind Turbine ››

A Horizontal Axis Wind Turbine is a electricity production plant that contains a number of subsystems, located in following areas: the Blades and Hub, the Nacelle, the Tower, and the Foundation.

•The Blades, that are attached to the Hub.

•The Hub itself, that is attached to Rotor Shaft.

The Nacelle is located on top of the Tower, and houses: 

•The Rotor Bearing, that support the weight of the Hub and Rotor Shaft.

•The Rotor Shaft that mechanically connects the Hub to the GearBox.

•The GearBox: a fixed ratio GearBox with two axis: the low speed one connected to the Blades through the Rotor Shaft and the Hub; and the high speed one, connected to the electric Generator through a flexible joint.

The elements above are known collectively as "DriveTrain".

•The Brake Disc, used to keep the position of the DriveTrain when standing,  and in exceptional safety conditions, to stop it. Sometimes, the Brake disc is located at the low speed axis of the GearBox, although in the most common case is located in the high speed axis.

•The flexible joint, that connects the high speed axis of the GearBox to the Generator's axis. This element solves the problems associated with the possible misalignment between the GearBox high speed axis and the Generator axis. It also behaves as a torque limiting device, to protect the Generator in the moments where the mechanical torque from the wind exceed the maximum torque that can be absorbed by the Generator.

•The electric Generator.

•The Wind Vane, used to provide information about how much is the disorientation of the main axis of the Nacelle (defined by the vertical plane that passes through the axis of the DriveTrain), and the wind direction.

•The Anemometer, to measure the wind speed.

•The Brake's hydraulic system, that actually control the status of the Brakes.

•GearBox's oil lubrication system, that normally include a high quality oil filtering system and a heat exchanger.

•Fans for the Generator and, eventually, for the GearBox's oil heat exchanger.

•A number of sensors for:

oTemperature measurements: for the Generator, the GearBox oil temperature, Rotor Bearing temperature, etc.

oRPMs of the two GearBox axis.

oPressures (in the Brakes hydraulic system)

The power cables connected to the Generator drops from the Nacelle through the center of the Tower during some distance, till they are attached to the Tower wall, then connect to the Electrical Panel, located at the bottom of the Tower.

Depending on the model, the Nacelle also houses:

•Capacitor banks, used to control the reactive power.

•The step up transformer, use to bring the Generator stator voltage from 690V to Medium Voltage (typically 15 KV), in order to reduces ohmic losses in the power cables. This transformer may also be located at the bottom of the Tower.
