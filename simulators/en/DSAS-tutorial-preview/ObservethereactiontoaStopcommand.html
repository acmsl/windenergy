A Wind Turbine is a production unit of electrical energy all by itself, and is designed in such a way that it does not depend on any external information in order to produce that energy, should the wind speed be enough, and if there is a suitable connection to the Grid system.

In that paradigm you can only:

• "ask it" to "start production" when suitable conditions are arrive. This is done with the "START" command.

or

• to "order it" to "stop production". This is done with the "STOP" command


The STOP command is obeyed immediately  following a very precise procedure, designed to minimize the efforts on the structures and wearing in the moving parts.


Although the Wind Turbine does have a braking mechanism that can be used to effectively reduce the turning speed of the Blades, that element is not used for normal stops of the machine. That would require big stopping pads with a very high heat dissipation capabilities. The use of the braking system is left for security purposes or as convenience tool to fixed the Blades during maintenance operations: similar to the purpose of a "parking brake" in a car. 


Normal stops of the Wind Turbine is achieve using the "air brake". It Wind Turbines with Pitch control, that is ready done modifying this pitch angle, the bringing the biggest surface of the blade normal to the rotational movement. In Wind Turbines with fixed pitch, it is common that the Blade's tip (the upper part of the Blade) can be rotated to that angle.

Taking into account the huge influence of the Pitch angle (the angle the chord of the blade respect to the rotating plane) (see the SynopticWindNacelle synoptic, right diagram) on the effective "aerodynamic coefficient" (Cp), this is definitively a very soft, clean and wear-less method to reduce the rotational speed of the DriveTrain.

If we consider the range of values of the aerodynamic coefficient (Cp), the factor that relates wind speed, DriveTrian speed and Pitch angle, and the characteristics of the To get acquainted of that influence you can check the values of the Cp (aerodynamic coefficient) as a function of the Pitch Angle and of the TipSpeedRatio that you can see here. The maximum theoretical value for Cp is around 0,5 , meanwhile in can reach down to -60 with pitch angles of -90º. Out of this big difference you can understand that the stop phase can be down much faster than the cutin phase.

You can observe the reaction to the STOP command. You should have the simulator in a production state (see how here), and then pressing the "STOP" (red) button in the "Control Terminal" synoptic.

You should see and evolution of the signals similar to the ones shown in next figures.

Ramp up and STOP with 6 m/s wind.


Notice the difference in the evolution of the RPMs both in the ramp up and cutout (STOP) with two different wind speeds: one (10 m/s) generates a mechanical torque that is 8 times the other (6 m/s) (Torque is proportional to the cube of the wind speed, so double speed means eight times more torque).

The stopping phase is faster with higher wind.
