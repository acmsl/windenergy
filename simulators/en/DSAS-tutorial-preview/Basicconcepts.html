Basic concepts


You can find in any of the books of the bibliography explanations on how to compute the energy from the wind. There are a few important details to keep in mind:

•Wind power  is proportional to the cube of the effective wind. I.e., the wind energy at 8 m/s is 8 times bigger than the energy at 4 m/s ( 8 = (8/4)3). Or the power at 9 m/s is 27 times bigger than the energy at 3 m/s ( 27 = (9/3)3).

•Wind power is proportional to the air density (the mass per cubic unit of the air). In turn the air density depends on the humidity, the atmospheric pressure and the temperature. Consider the wind as a set of small cubes of air moving: they have a mass and therefore, while they are moving, they have kinetic energy. This kinetic energy is proportional to that mass, hence, to the air density.  

•Wind power taken by the Blades are proportional to the area swept by these Blades, i.e, with the square of the diameter of the circle described by the tip of the Blades. A simple approach is to think that the Blades turning in front of the wind can be assimilated to a disc that (partially) stops the wind. It does not do it completely though. From the point of view of conservation of the total energy, the wind passing through the Blades has less energy that the wind arriving to the Blades: part of this lost energy is spent in moving the Blades, and all things attached to them (the DriveTrain and eventually the load represented by all the electric power delivered to the Grid.)
