Stalling


Besides the basic theory explained in "Why the Hub turns?" there is a phenomenon that is used in some Wind Turbines: stalling. It happens when the angle of wind incident in the edge of the blade and its chord is above a given value.

All the modern Wind Turbines have a mechanism to change this angle, the Pitch, in order to control the kinetic energy taken from the wind, in a way very similar to the control of flaps and position of wings in airplanes when approaching the runway. 

 The pitch angle is adequate to create a lifting force.

The pitch is very high and the lifting effect disappears.

Normal pitch in Wind Turbine controlled by "Active Pitch" for winds above nominal.

Solution of pitch, using specific blades designs, that can enter the stalling state with smaller angles. Case of the "Active Stall" method of control.



Next figure shows how the lifting and dragging forces depend on the Pitch angle. We have to remember that the dragging force is counteracted by the Tower, therefore from this figure it is clear that when the Blades enter the stalling region, the bending force on the Tower starts growing fast with the Pitch angle. This stalling characteristic is the base for the control (passive control) of the power in "fixed pitch" Wind Turbines.


This curve depends on the wind: i.e, given a fix Pitch, there is wind speed above which the stalling effect appears.

The "Dual Speed Active Stall Wind Turbine Simulator" is based in the "Active Stall" control method. The benefit of this method is that it lower the requirement on the Pitch mechanism. 

Comparing next two figures, we can see the big difference in the operational ranges of the Pitch for the cases of Active Stall and Active Pitch


While in the Active Stall case, the maximum variation is less than 5º, that happens in the 12 to 15 m/s range, for the Active Pitch case the change from 11 to 16.5 m/s is around 21º (green curve). Furthermore, in the Active Stall control method, for winds above nominal the Pitch remains practically constant.


On the other hand Wind Turbines that use the "Active Pitch" method are designed for remaining in the non stalling region, therefore  reducing the bending force on the Tower compared to the Active Stall method. In contrast, with Active Pitch requires a Pitch change mechanism with a more extended range, and consequently also faster rate, i.e more powerful.
