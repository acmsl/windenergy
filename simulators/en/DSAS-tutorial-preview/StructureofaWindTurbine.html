The Wind Turbine blades have similar requirements as airplane wings. In  Why the Hub turns you have an explanation on the physics involved in the movement of the Blades. In general, the best relation of "lift" force (the force that actual makes the Blades turn) versus "drag" force (the force that must be counteracted by the bending of the Tower) is obtained when the relation between the Blade's thickness to "chord" is around 10 to 15%. The most common types of their airfoil contour follow a pattern like the ones shown in next figure.


The next figure exemplifies why the section of the blade airfoil must twist along its longitudinal axis: the pitch angle at each section must keep a fixed difference from the total wind direction at that section (W).

As can be seen in the figure, the total wind (W) direction changes along the longitudinal axis of the blade, as the rotational wind (U) changes depending on the position of that section on that axis:

|U| = 2 * π * DistanceOfSectionToCenterOfHub 

Also, it can be appreciated that the modulus of the total wind (W) increments as we approach the tip: that is the reason for reduction of the airfoil section, in order to maintain an uniform load along the blade.
