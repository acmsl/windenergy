From the point of view of production, an aerogenerator is characterized by a "production curve"that gives the power it produces according to the incident wind.

This curve can have several forms, depending on the technology used in the wind turbine. The first industrial wind turbines had a curve as shown in Figure 1.

It can be observed that the power generated by increasing the wind from a certain wind value (about 16 m / s in the example of Figure 1) is reduced rather than increased. This behavior derives from thethe aerodynamic response of the shovel, which avoidspackagingof the Power Train when the wind rises above a certain value: i.e., this type of aerogenators includes a "per se" packing safety mechanism.

The difference with the above is that the rotation speed of the Power Train varies according to the wind, in order to have the best use of it. This requires that the conversion of the kinetic energy from the Power Train to electric power (the Electric Generator) be more sophisticated. In the above models, and precisely due to the type of electric converter (typically an asynchronous generator with short rotor - orsquirrel cage-), the Power Train is made to operate at an almost fixed speed (with variations of the order of 4%), which implies a non-optimal aerodynamic use at low wind speeds.

There are intermediate designs that appeared before the variable speed aerogenerators using two electric generators (or one, but with the possibility of two different electrical connections effectively simulating two generators) that have two operating speeds in production (two synchronous speeds), thus presenting a certain degree of adaptation to low winds. They are known as "two-speed".

This graph shows the expression "cutin"which corresponds to the concept of'start of the process of connection of the aerogenerator to the network and move to production'. Similarly, there is the concept of "cutout"which corresponds to the concept ofThe end of production and disconnection of the network'. The basic parameters are theNominal WindandNominal Power: this is the maximum power that is capable of producing the aerogenerator, which occurs when the wind is equal to or higher than the nominal.

It is important to bear in mind thatthe formThis curve depends on the air density, which also influences the value of the Nominal Wind, not the Nominal Power, which is limited only by the capacity of the electric generator.

The following table shows the description of the wind values indicated in the above graph.

V min for guidance

Under this wind level the Controller does not consider it worth guiding the Gondola.

V min for cutin

Below this value, the Controller does not start the network connection process.

V min change from large to small

For two-speed wind turbines, this value corresponds to the minimum value used by the "large" generator.

V max change from small to large

For two-speed wind turbines, this value corresponds to the maximum value used by the "small" generator.

V max for cutin

When the wind turbine is standing and the wind regime is very high, the controller will not start the cutin process if this wind is higher than this value.

V max continuous production

This wind value is the maximum in which the Controller keeps production on a continuous basis.

V max with time limit

When the wind exceeds, for a short period of time, the value of 'V max continuous production', the Controller shall maintain the production. However, if this value is exceeded over a prefixed period, the Controller will start the production output and network disconnection process (cutout).

V max absolute

If the wind reaches this value the Controller will immediately start the cutout process.
