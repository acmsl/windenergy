Why do the blades of a wind turbine turn?

🎯 Learning objective

After completing this section, you will understand the fundamental physical principle that enables the conversion of the kinetic energy of wind into rotational mechanical energy in a wind turbine.

Introduction: The role of the blades in a wind turbine

The blades are the key elements where energy conversion takes place in a wind turbine. Specifically, the blades transform part of the kinetic energy of the wind into rotational kinetic energy of the wind turbine's drivetrain.

How does this conversion take place?

This energy conversion occurs thanks to the aerodynamic forces generated when the wind passes through the blades. These forces cause the rotational motion of the rotor.

💭 Common misconception

At first glance, it might seem that wind forces simply "push" the blades perpendicularly to the plane of rotation, as if the wind were striking a vertical rotating wall.

However, this explanation is incorrect. The actual operating principle is far more sophisticated and efficient.

The real aerodynamic principle: Lift

The real reason why the blades turn is not intuitive, but it is fundamental to understand in order to grasp how a wind turbine works.

⚡ Key principle

Wind turbine blades operate on a principle similar to that which allows aircraft to stay airborne during flight.

This principle is based on the generation of lift forces produced by the aerodynamic profile of the blades.

Summary of key concepts

📝 Important points to remember:

  1. The blades convert wind kinetic energy into rotational energy
  2. This conversion is produced by aerodynamic forces
  3. The principle is NOT simply the wind "pushing" the blades
  4. The actual principle is similar to the lift that keeps an aircraft airborne
  5. The aerodynamic profile of the blades is essential for this operation
