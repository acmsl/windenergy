Comparison of cut-in in different wind turbine families

🎯 Learning objectives

Upon completing this experiment you will be able to:

  • Understand the cut-in (start-up) process in a wind turbine
  • Identify the differences in start-up behaviour between the three main families of wind turbines
  • Analyse the evolution of key variables during the transition from standstill to production
  • Create and analyse comparative charts of start-up time as a function of wind speed

What is cut-in or start-up?

📖 Concept: Cut-in (Start-up)

Cut-in is the process by which a wind turbine transitions from a stopped state (drivetrain at standstill and generator disconnected from the grid) to a production state (generating electricity and feeding the electrical grid).

This process involves multiple wind turbine subsystems and varies significantly depending on the technology used.

Importance of the comparative study

Each wind turbine family uses different strategies and technologies to perform the start-up. Understanding these differences will allow you to:

  • Evaluate the advantages and limitations of each technology
  • Understand the impact on energy production
  • Analyse maintenance and wear requirements
  • Optimise wind turbine selection according to site conditions

Experiment methodology

🔬 Experimental objective

Create a data table for each type of wind turbine relating wind speed to the time required to complete the start-up.

📊 Data table structure

For each wind turbine you will create a table with two columns:

  • Wind speed (m/s): From 6 m/s to 17 m/s in increments of 1 m/s
  • Start-up time (s): Time measured from the start until stable production

🛠️ Measurement tool: StripRecorders

You will use the StripRecorders (graphic recorders) functionality of the simulators to:

  • Visualise the time evolution of variables
  • Measure time intervals between events
  • Create active copies of measurements

📚 Consult the tutorial "Active copies" to learn how to use this tool.

Experimental procedure

📝 Experiment 1: DSAS wind turbine

Type: Squirrel cage induction generator directly connected to the grid with active stall control

Preparation:

  • Consult the tutorial "Setting the conditions for a Wind Turbine to start operation with low wind speed"
  • Familiarise yourself with the DSAS simulator controls
  • Identify the key variables to monitor

Execution:

  1. Start the DSAS simulator with a wind speed of 6 m/s
  2. Activate the StripRecorders for the main variables:
    • Rotor rotation speed (rpm)
    • Generated power (kW)
    • Generator status (connected/disconnected)
  3. Initiate the start-up process
  4. Observe and record the time from the start until stable production is reached
  5. Repeat the experiment increasing wind speed in steps of 1 m/s up to 17 m/s
  6. Record all times in your data table

📝 Experiment 2: RRC wind turbine

Type: Wound rotor induction generator with rotor resistance control and blade pitch angle control

Preparation:

  • Open the RRC simulator
  • Consult the tutorial "Cutin sequence with wind below nominal"

Execution:

  1. Repeat the same procedure as in experiment 1
  2. Pay special attention to:
    • The behaviour of the rotor resistance control
    • The movement of the pitch system
    • The evolution of the rotation speed
  3. Complete your data table for the RRC

📝 Experiment 3: DFIG wind turbine

Type: Doubly fed induction generator with pitch angle control

Preparation:

  • Open the DFIG (50Hz) or DFIG (60Hz) simulator according to your region
  • Consult the tutorial "Start up (cutin) with wind below nominal"

Execution:

  1. Repeat the procedure from the previous experiments
  2. Pay special attention to:
    • The behaviour of the back-to-back converters
    • The variable speed control strategy
    • The operating speed range
  3. Complete your data table for the DFIG

Results analysis

📊 Analysis activities

  1. Create comparative charts:
    • Plot the three "Start-up time vs Wind speed" curves on a single chart
    • Use different colours for each technology
  2. Analyse trends:
    • How does start-up time vary with wind speed?
    • Which technology starts up most quickly?
    • Are there critical speeds where behaviour changes?
  3. Compare technologies:
    • Which wind turbine has the smoothest start-up?
    • Which one generates the greatest mechanical stresses during start-up?
    • What impact does this have on maintenance?
  4. Reflect on applications:
    • At a site with variable winds, which technology would be most efficient?
    • How does start-up time affect annual energy production?

⚡ Key points for your report

Upon completing the experiment, make sure you can answer:

  • What are the main differences in the start-up process of each technology?
  • What advantages and disadvantages does each start-up method present?
  • How do the control systems (pitch, rotor resistance, converters) influence the start-up?
  • What relationship exists between technological complexity and start-up performance?
