  • Identify the factors monitored by the control system to decide when to yaw the nacelle

  • Analyse the yawing process and its operational limitations

Introduction: Why is yawing necessary?

📖 Fundamental concept: "Upwind" orientation

In "upwind" wind turbines (with the rotor positioned in front of the tower, facing the wind), the nacelle must remain oriented facing the wind to maximise wind energy capture.

If the nacelle is not correctly oriented:

  • The power captured from the wind is reduced
  • Lateral loads on the rotor and tower increase
  • Wear on mechanical components increases
  • The overall efficiency of the wind turbine decreases

The Yaw System

To keep the nacelle oriented towards the wind, wind turbines incorporate a yaw subsystem.

⚙️ Typical components of the Yaw system

The system typically includes two sets of motors with gearboxes, one for each direction of rotation:

  • Yaw motors: Provide the force to rotate the nacelle
  • Gearboxes: Reduce speed and increase torque
  • Ring gear: Mounted on the top of the tower
  • Drive pinions: Mesh with the ring gear to transmit motion
  • Yaw brakes: Keep the nacelle fixed when no yawing operation is taking place
  • Measurement systems: Sensors that determine nacelle orientation and wind direction

Factors monitored by the yaw process

The wind turbine controller continuously monitors several factors before deciding whether to yaw the nacelle:

1. Nacelle misalignment with respect to wind direction

📐 Methods for measuring misalignment

This value can be obtained in two different ways:

Method A: Using a wind vane

Uses a wind vane mounted on the nacelle:

  • The reference axis of the vane must be aligned with the vertical plane of symmetry of the nacelle
  • It measures directly the misalignment angle
  • Advantage: Direct and straightforward measurement of the orientation error
  • Disadvantage: Can be affected by turbulence created by the rotor itself

Method B: Using an advanced anemometer + Absolute orientation measurement

Uses absolute measurements with respect to geographic North:

  • An advanced anemometer provides the wind direction (with respect to North)
  • A measurement system provides the nacelle orientation (with respect to North)
  • The controller calculates the difference between both measurements
  • Advantage: Greater precision, less affected by local turbulence
  • Disadvantage: Requires that both the orientation measurement mechanism and the anemometer are correctly calibrated to North

⚠️ Importance of calibration

For Method B it is absolutely critical that both the nacelle orientation measurement system and the anemometer are properly calibrated and aligned to geographic North.

Any calibration error will result in a systematic orientation error in the wind turbine.

2. Current wind speed

The yaw system only operates when the wind speed is within an appropriate range, defined by two limits:

🌬️ Wind speed limits for yaw operations

  • 'Minimum for yawing' (Min for orientation):
    • If wind is below this value, the system does not execute yaw operations
    • Reason: With very low winds, the operation would consume more energy than it would gain
    • Additionally, at low winds the direction tends to be highly variable and unstable
  • 'Maximum for cut-in' (Max for cutin):
    • If wind exceeds this value, the wind turbine may be undergoing an emergency shutdown
    • Yaw operations are suspended for safety reasons

📚 For more details, consult this tutorial on wind limits.

3. Accumulated number of cable turns

🔌 The cable twisting problem

The power cables carry the generated electricity from the electric generator (located in the nacelle, which rotates) to the grid connection point (located at the base of the tower, which is fixed).

As the nacelle rotates following changes in wind direction:

  • The cables progressively twist around themselves
  • Mechanical tension builds up in the cables
  • There is a risk of damage from excessive torsion if a limit is exceeded

For this reason, the controller keeps a counter of the accumulated number of turns (positive in one direction, negative in the other). When a predefined limit is reached, the system must execute an untwisting operation.

📚 For more information about this process, consult the tutorial on nacelle yawing.

4. Wind turbine state

The behaviour of the yaw system depends on the operational state of the wind turbine:

🔄 Yaw strategy according to state

  • In Production state:
    • The wind turbine is generating electricity
    • Yawing is a priority to maximise energy capture
    • Misalignment thresholds tend to be stricter
  • In Stopped state:
    • The wind turbine is not generating
    • Yawing is less critical
    • Greater misalignment can be tolerated
    • This is the ideal time to carry out cable untwisting operations

Important operational considerations

⚡ Key aspects of operation

  • Yaw speed: Typically between 0.3 and 0.5 degrees per second (very slow compared to rotor rotation)
  • Energy consumption: Yaw motors consume electrical energy; unnecessary yawing reduces net production
  • Mechanical wear: Each yaw operation causes wear on gears, bearings and components
  • Design balance: The controller must optimise between:
    • Maintaining good alignment (maximum energy capture)
    • Minimising yaw operations (reducing wear and consumption)
    • Avoiding reaching cable twist limits

Analysis activities

    • What factors influence this decision?
  4. Parameter optimisation:
    • If a site has highly variable wind directions, how would you adjust the parameters?
    • If the wind is very stable in direction, what modifications would you make?

Key concepts to remember

📌 Summary

  • Correct nacelle orientation is essential to maximise energy capture
  • The yaw system is a trade-off between energy performance, mechanical wear and consumption
  • Multiple factors (misalignment, wind speed, cable twisting, wind turbine state) must be considered before deciding to yaw
  • There are two main methods for measuring misalignment, each with advantages and disadvantages
  • Cable twisting imposes important operational limits requiring untwisting operations
  • Correct calibration of measurement systems is absolutely critical
  • The operational state of the wind turbine significantly affects the yaw strategy
