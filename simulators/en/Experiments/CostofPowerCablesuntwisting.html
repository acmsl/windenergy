Cost of power cable untwisting

Introduction: Why is there a cost?

💰 The economic problem of untwisting

As we saw in the previous experiment, the power cables progressively twist as the nacelle rotates following changes in wind direction. When the twist limit is reached, the wind turbine must execute an untwisting operation.

This operation has several cost components:

  • Grid disconnection (cut-out): During this phase no electricity is generated
  • Untwisting rotation: The nacelle must rotate several full turns, a process that takes time
  • Grid reconnection (cut-in): Requires additional time until stable production is reached
  • Yaw motor consumption: These motors consume energy during the process

The objective of this experiment is to quantify these costs in economic terms.

Problem statement

📋 Analysis scenario

We will consider the following realistic scenario:

Initial conditions:

  • The wind turbine is in Production mode (generating electricity)
  • The power cables have reached the twist limit in one direction
  • Configured limit: 2.5 full turns (900 degrees)
  • A complete untwisting operation must be executed

System parameters:

  • Wind speed: Above rated wind speed (good production conditions)
  • Wind turbine Rated Power: 1,500 kW
  • Yaw motor consumption: 5 kW
  • Electricity selling price: €0.10/kWh
  • Yaw motor speed: 0.5 °/s

📝 Task 2: Calculate the untwisting time

Once disconnected, the nacelle must rotate to untwist the cables.

Theoretical calculation:

Total angle to cover:

If the limit is 2.5 turns in one direction, we must rotate 2.5 turns in the opposite direction

Angle = 2.5 turns × 360°/turn = 900 degrees

Motor speed: 0.5 °/s

Untwisting time:

t_untwisting = Total angle / Speed

t_untwisting = 900° / 0.5 °/s = 1,800 seconds = 30 minutes

Experimental verification (optional):

You can observe this process in the simulator if you modify the yaw speed parameters:

  • Set them to 0.5 °/s
  • Observe the actual time the complete process takes

Cost calculation

💶 Economic analysis

Once all times are obtained, we can calculate the total cost of the untwisting operation.

Total operation time:

T_total = t_cutout + t_untwist + t_cutin

T_total = _____ s + 1,800 s + _____ s = _____ seconds

T_total in hours = _____ s / 3,600 s/h = _____ hours

Cost components:

1. Lost production (revenue not obtained):

During cut-out, untwisting and cut-in:

Energy that would have been generated = Rated Power × Total time

E_lost = 1,500 kW × _____ h = _____ kWh

Lost revenue = E_lost × Price

Cost_lost_production = _____ kWh × €0.10/kWh = _____ €

2. Yaw motor consumption (direct cost):

Only during untwisting:

Energy consumed = Motor power × Untwisting time

E_consumed = 5 kW × (1,800 s / 3,600 s/h) = 5 kW × 0.5 h = 2.5 kWh

Consumption cost = E_consumed × Price

Cost_motors = 2.5 kWh × €0.10/kWh = €0.25

3. Total cost:

Total_cost = Cost_lost_production + Cost_motors

Analysis and reflection

🤔 Analysis questions

  1. Dominant cost component:
    • What is the main cost component: lost production or motor consumption?
    • In what proportion does each contribute to the total cost?
  2. Impact of wind speed:
    • How would the cost change if the untwisting occurred with wind below rated speed?
    • Would it be more economical to wait for low wind conditions to untwist?
  3. Untwisting frequency:
    • If the wind pattern causes one untwisting per week, what would the annual cost be?
    • What percentage of annual production would this represent?
  4. Optimisation:
    • What strategies could reduce this cost?
    • Would it be worth increasing the permitted turn limit? What risks would this involve?
    • Would faster yaw motors be beneficial? What considerations would need to be made?
  5. Comparison between technologies:
    • Is this cost the same for all types of wind turbines (DSAS, RRC, DFIG)?
    • What differences might exist in cut-out and cut-in times between technologies?

Practical considerations

🔧 Operational aspects in real wind farms

  • Smart planning: Modern SCADA systems can schedule untwisting during low-production periods (e.g., low night-time winds)
  • Weather forecasting: Forecasts are used to avoid untwisting just before periods of optimal winds
  • Continuous monitoring: Early warning systems when approaching the twist limit
  • Preventive strategies: Some controllers try to balance rotations in both directions to minimise accumulation
  • Adaptive limits: In some designs, the turn limit can be adjusted according to operational conditions

Conclusions

📌 Key points

  • Cable untwisting has a significant economic cost, mainly due to lost production
  • The cost is much higher when it occurs during optimal wind conditions
  • The untwisting time component is the longest part of the whole process
  • Yaw motor consumption is relatively small compared to lost production
  • A good management strategy can significantly minimise these costs
  • This is an example of how seemingly simple operational decisions have a direct economic impact on a wind farm
