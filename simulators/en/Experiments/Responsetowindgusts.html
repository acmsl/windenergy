Introduction: Wind as a complex phenomenon

🌪️ Characteristics of real wind

Wind is a complex atmospheric phenomenon that is difficult to model precisely. In practice, for wind turbine design and analysis, three fundamental parameters are considered:

  • Direction: Where the wind is blowing towards (e.g., North, South-West, etc.)
  • Mean speed: The speed averaged over a period of time (typically 10 minutes)
  • Turbulence: Rapid, irregular variations in speed and direction superimposed on the mean speed

From the perspective of structural and operational sizing of wind turbines, these characteristics are simplified into two main categories:

  • Mean wind: Relatively stable conditions
  • Wind gusts: Sudden and significant changes in speed

Characterisation of wind gusts

Wind gusts are characterised by several parameters:

📊 Gust parameters

  • Maximum speed: The peak speed reached during the gust
  • Minimum speed: The speed trough (may be below the preceding mean speed)
  • Amplitude: The difference between maximum and minimum speed
  • Maximum rate of change: How quickly wind speed changes (m/s²)
  • Duration: How long the gust lasts

The challenge of wind gusts

⚠️ Issues associated with gusts

Wind gusts pose two main problems that the wind turbine must solve:

1. Drive Train stability

  • Sudden changes in wind speed generate dynamic loads on the entire mechanical system
  • They can cause torsional oscillations in the main shaft
  • They accelerate component wear (bearings, gears)
  • In extreme cases, they can cause structural damage

2. Quality of power delivered to the grid

  • Variations in rotor speed translate into power fluctuations
  • These fluctuations can cause voltage variations in the electrical grid
  • The "flicker" problem: perceptible variations in the luminous intensity of lamps connected to the grid
  • Impact on the quality of electrical service for other consumers

General response strategies

🛠️ Compensation mechanisms

In general, compensation for changes in wind speed is achieved through adjustment of blade pitch angle. However, when dealing with gusts:

Fundamental limitation: The pitch mechanism may not be fast enough to compensate for the sudden speed changes characteristic of gusts.

For this reason, different wind turbine families have developed additional mechanisms to manage gusts, especially focused on:

  • Maintaining drive train stability
  • Ensuring the quality of power delivered to the grid

Response according to wind turbine family

Below is a description of how each technology addresses the problem of gusts:

⚙️ First-generation wind turbines: Fixed pitch and aerodynamic stall

Technology: Squirrel cage generator + Fixed pitch + Passive stall

Characteristics:

  • Blades with fixed pitch angle (not adjustable)
  • Squirrel cage generator directly connected to the grid
  • Passive control by aerodynamic stall

Gust response mechanism:

Drive train stability is achieved through the aerodynamic design of the blades:

  • When wind speed exceeds rated speed, the blades enter aerodynamic stall
  • In stall, the airflow separates from the blade surface
  • This causes an automatic reduction in the power captured from the wind
  • It acts as a "natural power limiter"

📚 See characteristic curve at: 'Fixed-Pitch Power vs Wind Speed'

Rotation speed control:

The rpm of the drive train are controlled by the characteristics of the squirrel cage generator directly connected to the grid:

  • The rotation speed is maintained very close to the generator's synchronous speed
  • Typical variation: no more than 1% of synchronous speed
  • 📚 More information: Introduction to induction machines and Generator characteristics

⚠️ Limitations of this technology:

  • Variable delivered power: Above rated speed, power decreases rather than remaining constant
  • Tower loads: The stall phenomenon increases the horizontal forces that the tower must withstand
  • Reduced efficiency: Under high wind conditions, not all the energy potential is exploited

⚙️ Wind turbines with active pitch control

Technology: Squirrel cage generator + Active variable pitch

Characteristics:

  • Blades with variable and actively controlled pitch angle
  • Squirrel cage generator directly connected to the grid
  • Hydraulic or electric system to adjust pitch

Gust response mechanism:

Above rated wind speed:

  • The control system adjusts blade pitch angle
  • Blades are progressively oriented towards feathering
  • This allows the excess wind to pass through without being captured
  • Maintains constant power at the rated value

📚 See characteristic curve at: 'Variable Pitch fixed-speed Power vs Wind Speed'

Rotation speed control:

Similar to the previous generation:

  • Squirrel cage generator directly connected to the grid
  • Rotation speed maintained very close to synchronous speed

✅ Advantages over the previous generation:

  • Constant power: Maintains rated power above rated wind speed
  • Lower tower loads: Feathering reduces horizontal forces compared to stall
  • Higher efficiency: Better wind utilisation across the full operating range
  • Controlled shutdown: Can stop the rotor in a controlled manner by turning blades 90°

⚙️ Wind turbines with rotor resistance control

Technology: RRC (Rotor Resistance Control) - Wound rotor generator + Variable pitch

Characteristics:

  • Blades with variable pitch towards feathering
  • Wound rotor generator (not squirrel cage)
  • Electronic device that allows insertion of variable resistance into the rotor circuit
  • High moment of inertia of rotor and drive train

Gust response mechanism:

This technology introduces an innovative concept: using the drive train as energy storage:

  1. During an ascending gust:
    • The pitch system cannot respond quickly enough
    • More power is captured from the wind than can be delivered to the grid
    • The excess energy is used to accelerate the rotor
    • The rotor acts as a flywheel storing kinetic energy
    • This requires the generator to operate at variable speeds
  2. During a descending gust:
    • The wind provides less energy than needed
    • The rotor decelerates, releasing stored kinetic energy
    • This helps to keep delivered power more constant

🔑 Key concept: The rotor as a flywheel

The moment of inertia of the rotor is sufficiently high for it to act as short-term energy storage.

The kinetic energy stored in a rotating rotor is:

E = ½ × I × ω²

Where:

  • E = Kinetic energy (J)
  • I = Moment of inertia (kg·m²)
  • ω = Angular velocity (rad/s)

A change in ω allows energy to be absorbed or released without sudden changes in delivered power.

Technological solution: Wound rotor generator

For the rotor to change its rotation speed while maintaining grid connection:

  • A wound rotor generator is used
  • A squirrel cage generator cannot be used (almost fixed speed)
  • An electronic device is incorporated that allows insertion of variable resistance into the rotor circuit
  • This allows control of the generator's slip

⚙️ Wind turbines with doubly fed generator (DFIG)

Technology: DFIG (Doubly Fed Induction Generator) - Variable pitch + Variable speed

Characteristics:

  • Blades with variable pitch towards feathering
  • Doubly fed induction generator (DFIG)
  • Back-to-back converters in the rotor circuit
  • Very wide operating speed range
  • Sophisticated control system

Gust response mechanism:

Similar to RRC, it uses rotor acceleration and deceleration to absorb/release energy and compensate for differences between the power captured from the wind and the power delivered to the grid.

✅ Advantages over RRC:

  • Much wider operating range: Can operate efficiently over a much wider wind speed range
  • More sophisticated control: Electronic converters allow precise and fast control
  • Higher efficiency: Optimises rotation speed for each wind speed
  • Better power quality: Independent control of active and reactive power

Proposed experimental activities

🔬 Recommended experiments

For each wind turbine family, carry out the following tests:

  1. Configure standard gust:
    • Use the gust model shown in Figure 1
    • Adjust parameters according to each simulator's specifications
  2. Monitor key variables:
    • Wind speed (input)
    • Rotor rotation speed (rpm)
    • Blade pitch angle
    • Generated power
    • Torque on the main shaft
  3. Observe and record:
    • Pitch system response time
    • Variation in rotation speed
    • Fluctuations in delivered power
    • Peaks of mechanical torque
  4. Tests with gusts under different conditions:
    • Gusts with low mean wind (below rated speed)
    • Gusts with high mean wind (above rated speed)

Comparative analysis

📝 Analysis questions

  1. On drive train stability:
    • Which technology shows the smallest variations in rotation speed?
    • Which technology shows the smoothest mechanical torque peaks?
    • How does the rotor moment of inertia affect the response?
  2. On power quality:
    • Which technology keeps power most constant during a gust?
    • In which cases would flicker problems be observed?
    • How does the DFIG improve power quality compared to RRC?
  3. On control mechanisms:
    • Is pitch sufficient to compensate for fast gusts?
    • What role does variable speed play in the response?
    • Why is the combination of pitch + variable speed superior?
  4. On design trade-offs:
    • What advantages justify the greater complexity of the DFIG?
    • In what scenarios would it be acceptable to use simpler technologies?
    • How does electrical regulation influence technology choice?

Key concepts

📌 Summary of fundamental concepts

  • Wind gusts are sudden speed changes that challenge wind turbine control
  • The pitch system alone may be insufficient to compensate for fast gusts
  • The rotor moment of inertia can be used as short-term energy storage
  • The ability for variable speed is key to exploiting rotor inertia
  • Each technology represents a trade-off between complexity, cost and performance
  • Technological evolution seeks to improve both mechanical stability and power quality
