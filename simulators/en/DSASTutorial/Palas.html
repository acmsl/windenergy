Blade angle adjustment mechanism (Pitch)

What is a wind turbine? ›› Structure of a horizontal axis wind turbine ››

The blade angle (pitch) is used to:

•Control the power taken from the air and transmitted to the power train. In production, this power is equal to the power delivered to the electrical grid, minus the power dissipated in friction and other losses.

•Stop the power train aerodynamically. The brake system is only used to secure the power train once it has stopped, or as a safety mechanism in case it cannot be stopped aerodynamically.

Additionally, in modern wind turbines, the angle of each blade can be modified independently for each blade. The reasons for having independent control for each blade are to compensate for the loss of wind that occurs when one of the blades passes in front of the tower, and also to compensate for the difference in wind speed that exists along the tower and up to the highest position that the blades can reach.

This difference in wind speed in the vertical direction is called wind shear, and depends on the location of the wind turbine, the roughness of the surrounding terrain, nearby buildings, wind direction, etc. An example of the vertical distribution of wind is shown in the following figure.

In wind turbines where the length of the blades (and therefore the height of the tower) is large, the difference in mechanical stress along them is significant.

Passing in front of the tower results in flicker in the electrical power produced if there is no compensation for the angle of passage. This is a parameter that is taken into account in relation to power quality.
