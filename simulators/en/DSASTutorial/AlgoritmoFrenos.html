Brake and signal control algorithm

Wind Turbine Simulator with Active Stall and Two Speeds ›› Synoptics used in this simulator ›› Power Train Synoptic (SynopticDriveTrain) ›› Common indicators ›› Brake system ››

The control algorithm used in the brake system is based on the following ideas:

1.Fail/Safe design principle: this principle establishes that a device that controls an important part of a system must be designed in such a way that, if it fails, it leaves the system in a "safe" position.

2. Enable rapid shutdown of the power train. This requirement stems from the secondary use of the brake mechanism as a "safety alternative" in the event that the power train cannot be slowed down by aerodynamic means.

3. Minimization of the number of sensors and actuators.

This power train brake mechanism is NOT the mechanism used for shutdown operations (cutout): these operations are performed using aerodynamic methods, i.e., using the blades, changing their angle of attack, or, in some older models that did not have a pitch change mechanism, by changing the shape. However, in exceptional conditions, for example, when the pitch mechanism fails, or certain RPMs have been exceeded in the power train, or a very rapid stop is required, the Controller also uses the hydraulic brake.

In the chosen design, the "rest" position is with the shoe pressed against the brake disc by the cylinder spring. This follows the 'Fail/Save' operating principle (if something goes wrong, the mechanism remains in a safe position), in this case the safe position is power train shutdown.

When the wind turbine is in production, the brake shoe is away from the disc, so it does not interfere with the movement of the power train. For this to happen, it is necessary to counteract the pressure of the spring in the cylinder that forces the brake shoe. This is achieved by increasing the hydraulic pressure applied to the base of the spring. This pressure increase is achieved by setting the valve to the 'closed' position and starting the pump.

When you want to brake (shoe against the disc), simply reduce the hydraulic pressure at the base of the spring. This is achieved by opening the valve. There is usually more than one valve, with different flow rates, so that the speed of the pressure drop, and therefore the braking, can be adjusted (to a certain extent).

Finally, in the design of this circuit, a minimum of elements are used: a pump, a reservoir for the liquid, the valve(s), the pressure switch (not shown), and the shoe separation sensor (not shown).

The condition of the brake shoe is not measured by any sensor, but is deduced by the controller during the start-up phase (cutin). The procedure consists of applying significant mechanical torque to the power train while keeping the hydraulic brake active (shoe against the disc). This mechanical torque is achieved by orienting the nacelle towards the wind and setting a pitch value that guarantees maximum wind utilization with low rotation values (i.e., with TSR practically 0). In these circumstances, the rotation speed of the power train is monitored: if it is not 0 (stopped), it is due to a problem with the shoe or the cylinder spring; in these circumstances, the cutin operation is aborted.

The pump is used to maintain the pressure at the base of the spring at a value that ensures that, in production, the shoe does not rub against the brake disc. To do this, this pressure is not measured, but rather a more robust device is used, a pressure switch, calibrated to the desired pressure. In this way, the controller only receives a digital signal (the output from the pressure switch) and has to control two other digital signals: the pump control and the valve(s) control. There is also a limit switch that is activated when the brake shoe is already separated from the brake disc. This signal corresponds to the signal associated with the 'Brake released' button. The pressure value at which this signal is typically activated corresponds to the 'yellow' mark on the circular hydraulic circuit pressure indicator.

The evolution of the signals related to this hydraulic circuit can be observed using one of the "Predefined recorder panels" (by clicking on the execution control synoptic: 'Synoptics' --> 'Recorders' a recorder form is displayed, and in 'Defined panels' select 'Hydraulic brake circuit'). You can observe the variation of various signals from the hydraulic circuit during different operating and stopping situations.

The basic control concepts are based on the hysteresis of the pressure switch, whose output is used by the Controller to start the pump when its value is 'Off', i.e., the pressure is below the lower calibration limit of the pressure switch.
