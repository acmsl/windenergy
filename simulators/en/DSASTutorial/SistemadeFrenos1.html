Functions and components of the brake mechanism

The mechanism for orienting the nacelle toward the wind (upwind) has the following functions:

•Orientation of the nacelle.
To do this, it has two sets of rotation motors, one for each direction, which are connected to a ring gear attached to the top of the tower. These motors are controlled from the controller, which implements the appropriate algorithms. The directions of rotation are referred to as CW (clockwise) and CCW (counterclockwise).
In maintaining this orientation, the following must be taken into account:

oThe orientation of the nacelle must be such that the wind direction is perpendicular to the plane of rotation of the blades most of the time, with minimal error. 

oThe energy required to rotate the nacelle and the blades attached to it.

oThe existing wind: if it is too low, it may not be important to ensure that the nacelle is oriented. These algorithms have to do with:

oMaintaining the orientation of the nacelle so that the wind direction is perpendicular to the plane of rotation of the blades most of the time.

oPreventing existing disorientation, as well as
  

•Maintaining the nacelle in a fixed orientation.
This is achieved with brake pads that secure the nacelle to the tower. These brakes are 

oDetecting and controlling the number of turns of the power cables that run down to the base of the tower from the generator. These cables fall freely for a few meters until they are finally fixed to the wall of the tower. The (limited) flexibility of these cables is used to solve the problem of the (power) connection between a fixed part (the electrical cabinet at the base of the tower) and a moving part (the generator, which rotates with the nacelle).
