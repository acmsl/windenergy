The purpose of the gearbox is to transmit the mechanical torque created by the blades connected to the hub to the electric generator shaft, adapting it to the rpm required by the generator.

The blades rotate at a low speed, between 10 rpm and 45 rpm (depending on the model and power), while the electric generator rotates between 1000 rpm and 2000 rpm, also depending on the model and power.

The following figure shows a cross-section of a gearbox: in this case, it contains three stages, the first of which is of the "planetary" type.

Fig. 1.

The input (on the left) is made by a device (planet carrier in green) that transmits power to the planets (in red). This device is supported by bearings (carrier bearings), which are on the housing. The planets are meshed, on one side, with the ring gear (toothed ring) that is fixed to the housing and therefore does not rotate, and on the other side, they move the sun (sun pinion) on the shaft of the intermediate stage (intermediate gear in blue). The other end of this stage moves the gear of the next stage, which moves the output pinion (high-speed pinion). The dark blue areas correspond to sections of the bearings that support the various rotating elements.

In the figure above, there are three planets; the following figure shows a more simplified view, but with four planets.

Fig. 2. (Courtesy of AeroTorque Inc.)

In this case, the power from the blades enters on the right.

The fundamental advantage of planetary stages is the increase in the contact surface for power transmission, proportional to the number of planets. 

The gear ratio corresponds to the product of the multiplication ratio of each stage. The values in wind turbines range from 1:50 to 1:120 in large wind turbines.

Another image that shows the simplified structure of a typical wind turbine gearbox is shown below. On the left is the left side view, cut at the "AA" plane.

The power input (connection to the hub) is on the left (rotor shaft), and the output (connection to the generator) is on the right (high-speed shaft). The rotation of the rotor shaft moves the three planetary gears: as the annulus is fixed to the structure and therefore does not move, this movement causes the three gears to move along the indicated circumference, thereby moving the central planetary gear, which is integral with the gear wheel that meshes with the second stage (middle shaft).
