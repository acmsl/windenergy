Functional block diagram of a wind turbine with a squirrel cage generator and variable blade angle.


The main functions of the controller correspond to controlling the connection (and disconnection) of the electric generator to the power grid and controlling the blade angle: this modifies the power obtained from the wind (when in production) and brakes the power train when in the shutdown phase.

Detailed functional diagram

This figure shows the functional diagram of this type of wind turbine.

Main functions

•Control of the connection and disconnection of the electric generator to the electrical grid: 

The controller monitors:

oThe wind (via the anemometer).

oThe revolutions of the generator shaft.

oGrid parameters (voltages and currents per phase).

and performs control via:

oThyristors.

oBypass contactors.

•Control of the power obtained from the wind:

The controller monitors:

oThe wind (via the anemometer).

and controls the power obtained from the wind through:

oThe blade angle control mechanism (pitch)

•Control of the two-speed generator connections

When the generator has two stator connection options available (meaning it can operate at two synchronous speeds), the controller automatically switches between these two speeds. The algorithm used is as follows:

oIf it is producing at high generator speed:

▪It initiates the process of disconnecting from the grid, modifying the blade angle so that the power obtained from the wind is reduced.

▪When the electrical power produced is 0, it disconnects the generator from the grid, opening the bypass contactors.

▪Maintains the blade angle so that the power train loses rotational speed until it reaches a value slightly below the low synchronous speed.

▪It changes the connection of the generator winding so that it corresponds to the low synchronism speed.

Auxiliary functions

•Operation safety control:

The controller has algorithms for monitoring the status of the wind turbine, whose mission is to ensure the safety of people and the equipment itself. 

To do this, it monitors:

oWind speed.

oThe speed range of the generator shaft.

oThe rotation speed of the blades.

oThe vibration sensor status of the tower.

oTemperature sensors for various elements: generator stator winding, power train shaft bearings, gearbox, environment.

and disconnects the grid (if connected), and sets the blade angle to "flag" (meaning minimum resistance to incident wind).

•Control of the nacelle orientation.

The controller uses the wind vane signal to determine the value of its disorientation with respect to the wind direction.

It uses the motor groups (one for each direction of rotation: CW (clockwise) or CCW (counterclockwise)) to correct any possible disorientation.

•Monitoring and control of the disc brake.

The disc brake is mainly used to prevent the power train from turning when maintenance operations need to be carried out on the blades, etc. It works like a handbrake in a car.

The components of the brake mechanism (shoe, disc, hydraulic cylinder, valve) are designed for another more demanding function: to act as a Power Train brake in emergency situations. When the Controller decides to stop the Power Train, it uses the blade angle, specifying an angle of 90º. If this procedure does not respond adequately (not fast enough), the disc brake is used as a last resort. This use is considered a failure because, if it is applied at production speed, all the energy obtained from the wind must be dissipated in the brake shoe, causing it to deform.

•Monitoring of tower vibrations

The controller reacts to the activity of the vibration sensor located in the tower, stopping the power train and placing the blade in a "flag" position.

•Hydraulic system maintenance.

The hydraulic system is used by the Power Train brake mechanism, but depending on the model, it can also be used to fix the rotation of the Nacelle, by means of shoes placed at its base. In certain models, it has also been used to supply pressure to the blade angle change mechanism.

The controller receives a signal from a pressure switch that allows it to know the pressure level in the hydraulic circuit. Depending on the operations to be performed, it starts and stops the hydraulic pump to maintain this value within an appropriate range, or acts on the relief valve to reduce this pressure.
