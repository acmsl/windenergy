Safety strategies for overspeed

Wind Turbine Simulator with Active Stall and Two Speeds ›› Scope of Simulation ›› Processes corresponding to Nacelle elements ››

One of the critical safety issues in wind turbines is the prevention of overspeed. Typically, the way to ensure this safety is to:

•Reducing the energy extracted from the wind by modifying the pitch angle of the blades. This is known as 'air braking'.

•Reducing the kinetic energy of the powertrain using the hydraulic brake, known as 'hard braking'.

If the evolution of the power train's rpm exceeds certain limits (which are above 'normal' production speeds), the controller attempts to reduce them using the above procedures. If this fails, the wind turbine has physical devices that attempt to act first on the electric generator shaft and, if this mechanism fails, on the blade shaft. These two procedures cause significant damage to the wind turbine, so they are only used in extreme cases, where it is preferable to cause this damage rather than personal injury or damage to objects outside the wind turbine.

In the case of the wind turbine used as a reference for this simulator, the Nacelle.GeneratorSpeedVCU generator shaft overspeed detector is activated at around 1803 rpm (in production, the maximum value is above 1520 rpm), and is a centrifugal sensor. 

If the power train exceeds this speed, three other sensors, located on each of the blades, are used and activated if the blade speed exceeds 25 rpm (in production, this value is less than 20 rpm).
