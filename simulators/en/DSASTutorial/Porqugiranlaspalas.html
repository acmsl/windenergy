Concepts about extractable wind power

Physical principles ››

"Why do the blades turn?" presents the basic concepts that explain why the force of the wind perpendicular to the plane of rotation of the blades causes them to turn. One consequence is that this rotational force depends on the shape of the blades and also on their angle relative to the plane of rotation: the greater the angle (pitch) (i.e., the less surface area of the blade "faces" the wind), the less force there will be. 

The formula that gives us the extractable power from the wind is as follows (see references in the Bibliography):

PowerExtractedFromWind =
0.5 * AirDensity * BladeSweptArea * Wind3 * Cp(Wind, BladeRotationSpeed, Pitch)

Where Blade Swept Area is π * Blade Radius², Blade Radius is the distance from the tip of the blade to the center of the hub, and Pitch is the angle of attack of the blade relative to the plane of rotation of the blades. 

The influence of Pitch is through the aerodynamic coefficient (Cp), which is a complex function whose theoretical maximum value is 0.59 (Betz's law) and which depends on:

•the wind, 

•the rotational speed of the blades, and 

•Pitch. 

It can also be seen that the influence of wind comes through the term Wind3, and also through Cp. 

The shape of the Cp function depends on the geometry of the blades, and, given a certain geometry, the value depends on three variables:

1. Wind speed.

2. The rotational speed of the blades.

3. The angle of attack (Pitch) of the blades. 

Fortunately, these three variables can be reduced to two by using a mathematical device that relates wind speed to blade rotation speed. This variable is known as TSR (Tip Speed Ratio: the ratio between the linear speed of the blade tip and the wind speed).

The definition of this auxiliary variable is as follows:

TSR = 2 * π * BladeRadius * (BladeRotationSpeed rpms)/60) / WindSpeed   

Using this TSR variable, the Cp function depends on only two variables: TSR and Pitch: Cp (TSR, Pitch)
