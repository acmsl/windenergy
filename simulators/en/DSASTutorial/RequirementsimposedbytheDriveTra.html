The maximum power that can be extracted from a given wind depends on the area swept by the blades. This means that this power is proportional to the square of the radius of the blade used.

The following figure shows the relationship between the maximum extractable power for a given type of blade and its radius. The x-axis represents wind speed (m/s) and the y-axis represents power (watts).

Fig.: Maximum power obtainable as a function of wind speed for various blade radius values.

The horizontal line above 2,000,000 watts indicates the power value we want to obtain: this value determines the characteristics of the electric generator we must use, and all its associated power electronics. It therefore corresponds to an upper limit that must not be exceeded, and is known as the nominal power.

In the figure, we see that this nominal power is reached with different wind values, depending on the blade radius: the wind with which we reach the nominal power is known as the Nominal Wind.

In other words, to determine the blade radius, we start from the desired nominal power and the wind available at our site.

Another very important conclusion from the figure above concerns the control of the Power Train. We can see that when the wind exceeds the nominal wind speed, the power obtainable is much greater than the nominal power. However, the electric generator has been designed for the nominal power. This means that we must have a mechanism that allows us to reduce the power that is actually extracted from the wind, so that it does not exceed the nominal power. This mechanism is the control of the blade angle (pitch).

In addition, wind has other characteristics besides the 'stable' Weibull distribution, such as gusts and turbulence, which take into account the rate of change in wind speed. The pitch control mechanism must be sized to withstand the maximum wind gusts expected at the wind turbine location, especially considering that the blades are quite heavy and that pitch changes occur while the blades are rotating, resulting in additional gyroscopic effects that must be overcome.
