Pt100 temperature sensors are devices that contain a platinum resistor, which comes into contact with the element whose temperature is to be measured. It is a very reliable type of sensor that is widely used in industry due to the stability of the relationship between resistance and temperature. The resistance curve as a function of temperature is also fairly linear in the range of use.

There are several values: the Pt100 type has a resistance of 100 ohms at 0°C and a proportionality coefficient of 0.385 Ohms/°C. In other words, the resistance value when the sensor is placed on an object at 100°C is 138.5 ohms (= 100 ohms + 100°C * 0.385). 

Measuring this resistance presents two problems: one derived from the low value of the variations with temperature combined with the influence of the resistance of the wires connecting the sensor to the device performing the measurement. The solution is to use a measurement circuit such as the one shown in Fig. 1: R1, R2, and R3 correspond to the resistances of the wires connecting the RPT sensor to the measuring device (located at terminals L1, L2, and L3).

By design, the values of R1, R2, and R3 are very similar. If the resistances between terminals L3 and L2 are measured, and then between terminals L2 and L1, the resistance of RPT is the difference between the two.

A similar concept is used with the 4-wire method.

The idea is based on the fact that any voltage measuring instrument has a high impedance (greater than 1 M ohms) and therefore, if a known current is injected between terminals L1 and L4, only a negligible part will flow through L3 and L2, so that the voltage measured on the voltmeter (DVM) will be the same as that at the RPT terminals.

The other problem stems from the fact that injecting current into the RPT to take the measurement heats up the RPT, thereby distorting the measurement. This error depends not only on the current, but also on the thermal characteristics of the RPT and the point where the measurement is taken.

To solve this problem, some systems use a short current pulse mechanism to take the measurement.
