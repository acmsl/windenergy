Functional Structure of the Controller

The controller of a wind turbine is a concept that encompasses the processes of signal monitoring and control of elements to achieve the goal of maximizing the energy produced, while maintaining the necessary safety mechanisms.

Its implementation varies greatly, and, in general, controllers are implemented with one or more PLCs (Programmable Logic Controllers) or through industrial computers, usually running real-time operating systems (RTOSs).

PLCs are monitoring and control units that do not have an operating system and are programmed to implement the logic of certain processes.

Industrial computers are computers created for industrial environments and where real-time operating systems are run: unlike other common operating systems for office applications, RTOSs guarantee known response times for their operations. This feature is essential to be able to handle certain operations in industrial plants, which must be carried out in a short time and must not exceed certain limits.

The algorithms implemented must correspond to the different subprocesses, those for general coordination between them, and those for responding to 'external' commands (both from the operator, via a local connection, and from the control center, via a remote connection).

Examples of subprocesses are: 

•The orientation of the nacelle.

•Maintaining pressure in the hydraulic brake circuit.

•Control of the electric generator cooling fans.

•Etc.

Coordination and safety functions are usually programmed using the "state machine" paradigm. This programming method has several advantages, including the ability to visualize the different possible evolutions (state transitions) and the possible reasons for each of these evolutions (events).

In general, there are two state machines in wind turbine control, one to implement the operation functions and another to implement the safety functions.
