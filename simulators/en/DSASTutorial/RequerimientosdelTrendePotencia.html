Definition of wind

The wind regime of a location is described as a statistical function called "Weibull." The following figure shows on the y-axis the percentage of time (in hours per year) that the wind is in the range indicated on the x-axis.

At the site corresponding to the distribution in the figure above, the most probable wind speed is between 4 and 6 m/s.

The Weibull distribution has two characteristic parameters: the "average wind" and the "highest expected wind." 

However, as we are interested in the power that can be extracted from the wind, it is more interesting to consider the distribution of the power associated with the wind, for which we must apply the formula described in Concepts on extractable wind power.

WindPower = K * AirDensity * BladeSweptArea * Wind3   

Where K is a constant. If we apply this formula to the previous graph to obtain the power distribution as a function of wind, we get the following figure. The x-axis continues to refer to wind speed (m/s) and the y-axis to power.

We can see that, in this particular case, although the wind speed that has the most hours per year is between 4 and 6 m/s, there is a higher production speed, around 10 m/s, so this value becomes the "Nominal Wind."

The second condition defines the characteristics of the electric generator to be used in the wind turbine, and all the associated power electronics (if any). This value is the "Nominal Power."

The length of the blades must be such that at the nominal wind speed we have the Nominal Power. This condition has an important consequence: for winds higher than the nominal wind, the power that can be extracted from the wind is greater than the nominal power of the electric generator. This very important aspect is presented in the following paragraph: Power Train Requirements.
