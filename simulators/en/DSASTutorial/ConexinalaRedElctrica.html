Connection circuit details

The following figure shows a detail of the connection of the electric generator to the grid.

Fig 1

The main components are:

1. The "main switch" that connects to the grid, on the left of the figure.

2. The thyristor block and its bypass contactors, which are part of the soft starter, the mechanism for smooth connection to the grid.

3. The thyristor temperature sensors.

4. The contactor block for changing the generator connection between 4 and 6 poles.

5. The controller, which monitors the status of these elements and generates control signals for them.


Important details:


•The "Main Switch" can be turned on or off manually. When it is on, it can be turned off from the Controller using the "Main Circuit Breaker Reset" signal. This occurs when the Controller executes a safety sequence.

•The contactors have a mechanism for reporting their status to the Controller, consisting of two contacts connected to the main contactors: one labeled "NC" and the other "NO." The wire connected to the "NO" terminal is active (has voltage) when the contactor is in Off mode, while the "NC" is active (has voltage) when the contactor is in On mode. These two signals reach the Controller and are used to:

oKnow the status of the contactor (open or closed).

oKnow if there is an error in the circuit: that is, if there is a broken wire or the corresponding contacts in the contactor are damaged, etc. The logic of this detection is explained in Contactor problem detection logic.

•The thyristors have bimetal temperature sensors, which open when the temperature exceeds a certain value. These sensors are connected in series to the "Over Temp" input of the Controller, so that this signal is "closed" if the temperature in all the thyristors is below the limit, and "open" if any of the thyristors has exceeded that limit.

•The "Gate Control" signal is actually 6 signals that are applied to the gates of the corresponding thyristors, as explained in Soft starter (soft connection) to the mains.
