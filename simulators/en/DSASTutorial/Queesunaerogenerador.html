What is a wind turbine?

A wind turbine is an electricity generation plant capable of transforming the kinetic energy of wind into electrical energy.

It can be described as two power converters working together: the first transforms the linear kinetic energy of the wind into rotational kinetic energy, and the second transforms this rotational energy into electrical energy.

The first converter is called the "DriveTrain" and consists of blades, which oppose the passage of the wind, mounted on a device that allows them only one degree of freedom (rotation). In this way, the thrust of the wind is transformed into the rotation of this DriveTrain. 

The second converter consists of an electric generator whose rotor is connected to the DriveTrain. In most cases, this connection is made through a gearbox, because the optimal working conditions of the two converters, basically the revolutions of their shafts, do not coincide.

There are several types of electric generators. The most common are: 

•asynchronous with squirrel cage, 

•asynchronous with access to the rotor winding, 

•synchronous, and 

•synchronous with permanent magnets. 

Depending on the type of electric generator, wind turbines have additional power electronics equipment for their operation. 

The electric generator causes the electrical power sent to the grid to appear as mechanical resistance on its shaft, which opposes the rotation of the power train.  

 In the case of the wind turbine type corresponding to this simulator, the generator is of the "squirrel cage" type, with the stator winding directly connected to the grid.

There are several types of wind turbines, depending on the position of the shaft around which the blades rotate: vertical and horizontal. Those installed in wind farms are almost all horizontal axis. In English, they are known by the acronym HAWT (Horizontal Axis Wind Turbine). This type of wind turbine has an orientation mechanism that keeps the plane of rotation of the blades in a perpendicular position.

Types of wind turbines

Wind turbines can be classified according to these two converters. The most common in industrial use is the HAWT (Horizontal Axis Wind Turbine): the blades rotate in a vertical plane, on an axis that remains horizontal; the wind (parallel to the surface of the land) strikes perpendicularly on the plane of rotation of the blades. The axis is known as the "power train," rotates horizontally, and is located on an elevated platform known as the "nacelle."

The plane of rotation of the blades must be kept perpendicular to the wind. This plane can be the front of the nacelle (the blades are the first thing the wind hits) (upwind) or the rear (downwind). The theoretical advantage of the downwind method is that the wind itself is responsible for orienting the nacelle; however, it has the disadvantage of losing wind, which collides with the nacelle and the tower before reaching the blades. For this reason, the vast majority of HAWT wind turbines are oriented upwind. This means that they must have a mechanism to keep them properly oriented "facing" the wind.

They generally have three blades, although there are industrial models with two blades.

As for electrical converters, they all use some type of electric generator and, in some cases, power electronics equipment. 

The most common types of electric generators are:

•Asynchronous induction generator.

•Permanent magnet induction generator. 

When asynchronous induction generators are used, it is necessary to use a gearbox, since the power train normally rotates at speeds of around 10 to 40 rpm, while asynchronous induction generators operate in the range of 1000 to 2000 rpm.

In cases where permanent magnet induction generators are used, there are some designs that allow them to operate directly at the speed of the power train, and therefore do not require a gearbox.
