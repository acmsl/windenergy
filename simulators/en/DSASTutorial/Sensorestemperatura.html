Temperature sensors

Wind turbines have temperature sensors for different elements. The type of sensor depends on the type of component being monitored and the required measurement accuracy. For example, to measure the temperature of thyristors or yaw motors, bimetal-based sensors calibrated for the corresponding temperature are generally used. These sensors are in a closed circuit when the temperature is lower than the calibration temperature and in an open circuit if it is higher.

Other elements, such as the electric generator or the rotor bearing, require more accurate temperature measurement so that their evolution can be monitored more closely.

More accurate measurements that require greater reliability are performed using Pt100 sensors, which are widely used in industry and have well-known and stable performance and an adequate level of accuracy.

The physical effect used is the variation in the resistance of a conductor as a function of its temperature. These sensors are made of platinum and their nominal value is small, typically 100 ohms, although 1,000 ohm sensors are also available. 

The relationship between resistance and temperature is quite stable in platinum and follows a reasonably linear rule.

The main problem with this type of sensor is the measurement of its resistance. As it has a low value and is at a considerable distance from the electronic device that performs the measurement, it is necessary to avoid the problem of the cables connecting the Pt100 sensor and its measurement system, and also to reduce the current used in the measurement as much as possible, as this current 'heats' the resistance and therefore distorts the measurement.

The procedure, well known in industry and laboratories, is known as the 3- or 4-wire method. See Pt100 sensor measurement circuits.
