Connection to the Electrical Grid

What is a Wind Turbine? ››

The connection to the electricity grid is where the electrical energy produced by the wind turbine is evacuated. In reality, this connection is made in two sections, one connecting the wind turbine to the wind farm's electrical substation where the wind turbine is located, and the other connecting the substation to the electricity grid itself.

Both sections have specific operating voltages. The connection of the wind turbine to the substation is usually made through a transformer, located at the foot of the wind turbine or inside it, whose secondary winding is at a voltage in the medium voltage range (over 1,000 V), typically between 15 kV and 22 kV. The primary is typically 690 V, which is the voltage of the electric generator used.

The electric generator used in the wind turbine on which this simulator is based is a so-called "squirrel cage" or "short-circuit rotor" generator (see details). The electrical power generated is output through the stator connection.

Connection of the wind turbine generator to the transformer

The connection is made via power cables that run down from the nacelle to the electrical cabinet located at the bottom of the tower. 

Generator operating modes

In the model used in this simulator, which allows operation at two speeds, this is achieved by using two different ways of connecting the stator windings. These are designed so that one stator connection generates 4 magnetic poles (2 pairs of poles) and the other generates 6 poles (3 pairs of poles). This means that the synchronous speed in one case will be 1500 rpm (for a 50 Hz network) and in the other case 1000 rpm. 

Synchronism_speed = (50 Hz * 60 seconds / Pairs_of_Poles)

As the wind turbine achieves its highest power at 1500 rpm, this connection is usually called "Large Generator," while the 1000 rpm connection is called "Small Generator."

In some older models, the same functionality was achieved by using two independent generators connected to a gearbox with two high-speed "output" shafts, each with a different ratio to the low-speed shaft.

In the case of a 60 Hz grid, the speeds of a similar generator would be 1800 rpm and 1200 rpm, respectively.

Details of the Electrical Panel

The connection of the cables coming down from the electric generator to the cables coming out of the Electrical Panel and connecting the wind turbine to the Wind Farm Common Connection Point (known as PCC) is made through the Panel busbars, the protection fuses, and the Main Contactor of the wind turbine. 

The busbars of the Panel are equipped with instruments for measuring the voltages in the phases and the currents in each phase: the current measurement per phase is carried out through current measuring transformers inserted in the bars. The output of these current measuring transformers is standardized at 1 Amp or 5 Amps full scale: therefore, their primary/secondary ratio (e.g., 1000/5) must be known, which is a parameter that must be entered into the Controller to perform the correct calculations. The power cables are connected through a circuit composed of 3 groups of counterphase thyristors that have a contactor in parallel, known as a bypass. This group of components, together with a control logic that generates the thyristor trigger signals (which generates the signals that go to the thyristor gates), are used to achieve a soft connection. This connection mechanism is known as SofStarter.
