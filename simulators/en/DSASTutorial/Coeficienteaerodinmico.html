The Cp function (see Aerodynamic coefficient) shows the complexity of the relationship between its value and the TSR values (which in turn depend on the wind, the blade radius, and the blade rotation speed) and the blade angle (Pitch). However, if we look for the maximum Cp value for a given TSR value among all possible Pitch values, we obtain a curve as shown in the following graph. This curve is dependent on the shape of the blade, logically.

The interest of this graph lies in the fact that it shows an absolute maximum: that is, there is a TSR value for which the Cp is maximum. It seems clear that when the wind is lower than the nominal value, what matters is that the TSR at which we should operate the wind turbine corresponds to that point (in the graph in the figure, thisoptimal TSR is around 7.8 m/s), since then we will be obtaining the greatest power from the wind. 

Remembering that the definition of TSR is:

TSR = 2 * π * BladeRadius * (BladeRotationSpeed rpms)/60)/ WindSpeed

the above statement implies that we should operate the power train at a speed that depends on the incident wind:

Optimal Blade Rotation Speed rpms = 60 *Optimal TSR * Wind Speed /( 2 * π * Blade Radius)

An important consequence of rotating the blades at this speed is that the maximum power extracted from the wind in this case is proportional to the square of the wind speed (Wind2). This graph also shows the desired Nominal Power (target) value for the wind turbine: this value corresponds to the Electric Generator specification. 

The minimum wind speed at which we want to obtain the Nominal Power is the Nominal Wind Speed. In other words, the nominal wind and power values determine the radius of the blade.

Below the Nominal Wind, the power obtained from the wind will be less than the Nominal Power. Above this wind speed, the power may be greater than the Nominal Power. As the electrical component of the wind turbine will be dimensioned to provide the Nominal Power, the wind turbine must have a mechanism that ensures that above the Nominal Wind, the power extracted from the wind does not exceed the Nominal Power. See Control of the power collected from the wind.

IMPORTANT CONCLUSIONS:
1- In order to always obtain the maximum power available from the wind in winds below the Nominal Wind Speed, the Power Train must operate at variable speed. This is an important condition for the electric generator.
2- The Power Train must include mechanisms or designs that ensure that the mechanical power delivered to the electric generator does not exceed its Nominal Power.
