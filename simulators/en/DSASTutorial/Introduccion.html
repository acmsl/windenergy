Introduction

This tutorial corresponds to the "Two-Speed and Active Stall Wind Turbine Simulator" from the ACM SL family of Real-Time Wind Turbine Simulators.  Its design models wind turbines that use squirrel cage electric generators, i.e., with short-circuited rotor windings and the stator connected directly to the grid. The power generation control system is achieved by varying the angle of the blade in relation to the wind (pitch), using the active stall principle (induced aerodynamic losses). It also implements the concept of adaptation to the wind range by switching between two generators (or a single generator with two windings).

This simulator runs on the LdP platform from ACM SL, using the "DSASr12.conf" configuration, which utilizes a set of LdP components (see Wind Turbine Simulator with Active Stall and Two Speeds).

With this Simulator, you can execute the commands supported by a real wind turbine and observe its reactions. Each of the wind turbine's subsystems has its own simulator, with specific parameters and signals that can be observed. Using specialized synoptics, the values of these signals can be modified (even during the execution of the simulation), so that the simulator can be used as a "Test Bench." The design of the subsystem simulators and synoptics allows the evolution of these signals to be observed under general operating conditions similar to those faced by a real wind turbine in a wind farm.

For example, the reaction to extreme winds can be observed. Or how it reacts to excessive heating of the generator. Or to a breakage of the blade angle variation mechanism. Or the influence of increased weight on the blades (due to ice, for example).

The simulated wind turbine is a HAWT (horizontal axis) type, wind-oriented (upwind), with three blades.

This tutorial explains the physical principles used in the design of wind turbines that use the principle of power control through stalling.

The objectives of this simulator are:

•To emulate the behavior of each subsystem within the wind turbine, including the controller, so that the observed behavior is very close to that of the actual wind turbine.

•To provide the user with a set of visualization tools with which they can effectively and easily observe the values of all internal signals and their evolution.

•To allow interaction with the wind turbine by changing its environmental conditions (wind, connection to the power grid, temperatures, etc.) in order to introduce most of the real operating conditions.

•Present, in a simple way, aspects related to the safe operation of wind turbines.

•Act as a "test bench" where the following can be forced: a) external conditions (extreme winds, for example), b) internal conditions (connection failures, equipment malfunctions, etc.), and observe the reaction of the wind turbine, even for signals that are not accessible in real wind turbines.

As a result, this simulator, together with this tutorial, allows:

•Learn about and explore the internal structure of this type of wind turbine.

•Understand the physical principles on which industrial wind turbines installed in wind farms are based.

•Learn about the algorithms used in each subsystem of the wind turbine and the parameters that characterize them.

•Learn about the state machines used to control the wind turbine.
