Power Train Brake System

The power train, which includes the blades, has a brake mechanism that is NOT (normally) used to reduce the speed of the blades, but rather as a mechanism to fix their position once they have stopped (or are rotating at very low speed). To reduce the rotational speed of the blades, the value of their pitch angle is used: above a certain value, the blades push the air, transferring kinetic energy from the power train to the air.

Brake concepts in a wind turbine

The power train of a wind turbine can be in the following situations:

•In production
In this state, the angle of the blades causes energy to be collected from the wind in the form of mechanical torque that moves the electric generator shaft, which transforms it into electrical energy.

•Idle
In this state, the angle of the blades is such that they do not collect any energy from the wind, so the power train does not rotate. This angle is 90 degrees and this position is called "flagged."
In this position, the aerodynamic coefficient of the blades is negative: that is, any rotation that the power train may make is braked by the blades. In other words, any rotational energy that the power train has is used to "move the air," and therefore, it is energy that is transferred to the air and thus decreases.
In this state, the power train can rotate, although for a short time.

•Braking
This state is used to perform work inside the wind turbine and to ensure the safety of personnel. This state is ensured by a disc brake system: see Some industrial examples 

Functions of the brake system

The brake system in a wind turbine has the following functions:

•Prevent the power train from moving while in the "parking" position, typically during maintenance operations.

•Prevent overspeed of the power train.
The controller continuously monitors the rotational speed of the power train and initiates a safety protocol to ensure that it does not exceed a maximum value.
To do this, it uses two devices:

oThe blades, setting an angle such that the mechanical torque of the wind is negative, i.e., it brakes the power train, which corresponds to an aerodynamic brake.

oIf the aerodynamic brake is unable to reduce the rotational speed of the power train within a certain time, the controller will use the disc brake system.

Stopping the power train involves dissipating the kinetic energy stored in it. Therefore, if the disc brake system is used, it can result in significant deformation of the disc or brake pads, which constitutes a major failure of the wind turbine.

The brake disc can be located on the high-speed shaft of the gearbox (i.e., on the generator shaft) or on the low-speed shaft (i.e., on the rotor shaft, which is directly connected to the blade hub). The most common position is on the high shaft, since, although there is usually more space on the low shaft, that position requires greater friction torque.
